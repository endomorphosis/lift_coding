## Numerical primitive

The new [masked loss owner](../../external/ipfs_datasets/ipfs_datasets_py/logic/formalization/autoencoder/alignment_masked_contrastive.py) exports `masked_multi_positive_contrastive_loss(source_embeddings, formal_embeddings, *, positive_mask, permitted_negative_mask, temperature=0.07, negative_weights=None)` and `describe_masked_contrastive_relations(positive_mask, permitted_negative_mask)`. Import requires only the standard library; calling either numerical interface requires the optional PyTorch runtime.

Source vectors have shape `N × D`, formal vectors `M × D`, and both masks `N × M`. The source and formal projections must already share a representation space. Matching raw widths alone do not establish that alignment. The operator accepts CPU float32 or float64 representations and dense CPU boolean masks, with at most 128 anchors on each side and width at most 8,192. It does not normalize, pad, project or reinterpret vectors.

Each source anchor and each formal anchor must have at least one positive. Positive and permitted-negative masks must be disjoint. A pair in neither mask is unknown and contributes to neither the numerator nor denominator in either retrieval direction. Optional fixed negative weights are finite and at least one at permitted negatives. Values at positive and unknown entries are ignored; exclusion uses an explicit mask rather than a zero negative weight.

For logits `z = source @ formal.T / temperature`, the source-direction loss is the difference between the log-sum-exp over permitted denominator entries and the log-sum-exp over positives. Permitted negatives receive their fixed log-weight in the denominator. The formal direction uses the same construction down columns. The scalar is the average of the two directional means, including anchors with no permitted negatives. Stable reduction follows PyTorch's [log-sum-exp operation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/generated/torch.logsumexp.html).

| Pair or anchor condition | Numerical behavior | Evidence meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caller positive mask | Positive numerator and denominator contribution. | Caller selection; admission must be established elsewhere. |
| Caller permitted-negative mask | Denominator contribution with optional fixed weight. | Caller selection; differing IDs or targets are insufficient evidence. |
| Unknown pair | No objective contribution; zero derivative with respect to that pair's logit. | Retained uncertainty. |
| Anchor without permitted negatives | Zero directional separation signal; remains in the directional mean. | Reported explicitly rather than treated as a useful training example. |
| Entire batch without permitted negatives | Differentiable exact zero loss and representation gradients. | No separation signal. |
| Anchor without positives | Rejects. | No fabricated default positive. |

Exclusion concerns a pair's logit. A representation participating in other permitted pairs can still receive gradients. Changing a whole vector can change those other logits; a test of exclusion must isolate the unknown logit rather than assume every gradient into that vector should vanish.

All inputs and computed pair logits must be finite, including unknown pairs. That numerical domain check does not make unknown pairs training examples. Finite forward values do not universally guarantee finite gradients for arbitrary unnormalized vectors and very small temperatures. A future fit worker must check its parameter gradients before any update. Gradient evidence here concerns the named engineering fixtures.

The descriptor reports exact mask digests, positive/permitted-negative/unknown counts, per-direction coverage and anchors without negatives. It retains pending verification/admission, five zero supervision masks and false semantic/proof/production authority. It does not create a training policy or infer that a caller's boolean choices are reviewed truth.

## TRAIN relation declarations

The new [relation declaration owner](../../external/ipfs_datasets/ipfs_datasets_py/logic/formalization/autoencoder/alignment_relation_declarations.py) exports `validate_relation_declaration(declaration, *, expected_bindings)`. This standard-library interface binds four separately selected sealed roles: the existing lane validation receipt, complete TRAIN manifest, diagnostic relation policy and complete ordered pair ledger.

The TRAIN manifest joins the lane receipt's ordered IDs, exact source/context hashes, vector bindings and availability. Each of the `N²` ledger rows binds both TRAIN IDs, input hashes and formal-view digests. Rows must follow the selected TRAIN order in both directions. External role pins cover complete sealed values and ordered rows; local resealing cannot substitute another selected input or policy.

Relation statuses are `unknown`, `declared_positive`, `declared_negative` and `weak_structural_identity`. Exact formal-view identity permits a weak structural declaration. It does not establish that either formal view faithfully translates its source, or supply a reviewed semantic positive. Declared positive/negative evidence hashes and family/profile names remain unverified metadata. Contradictory or asymmetric declarations do not acquire authority through this owner.

Every input pair mask must be the exact boolean `False`. The returned positive and permitted-negative matrices remain entirely false, all admitted counts are zero, and eligibility is `blocked_no_admitted_relations`. No operation in this version turns a declaration into admitted supervision. Family coercion, evidence authentication, semantic equivalence and independent review remain unperformed. A weak diagnostic fit would need a separate explicit policy.

The loss primitive and declaration validator intentionally have different readiness conditions: the numerical primitive requires positives, while the current unadmitted declaration receipt supplies none. The current TRAIN readiness cannot be passed to that primitive as a qualified semantic batch. Later authenticated admission needs a separately versioned owner and an exact relation-to-mask handoff.

## Historical readiness and engineering canaries

The [readiness runner](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/assay_relation_readiness.py) consumes the pinned preceding native384 TRAIN declaration and its independently selected role bindings. It reads source/input/formal-view digests, rather than formal target bodies or query references. The saved [relation assay](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/masked-contrastive-01/relations/assay.json) preserves all 16 TRAIN rows and 256 ordered pairs: 16 weak structural identities and 240 unknown relations, with no admitted positives or negatives. Original weak decoder masks remain unchanged.

The separate [numerical canary runner](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/assay_masked_contrastive.py) uses authored engineering tensors only. Its [saved numerical evidence](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/masked-contrastive-01/numerical-02/numerical_assay.json) exercises old-loss parity, explicit exclusion, rectangular multi-positive arithmetic, zero-signal batches, finite gradients into both untrained fixture heads and double-precision finite-difference checking. PyTorch documents the purpose and double-precision assumptions of [gradcheck](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/main/generated/torch.autograd.gradcheck.gradcheck.html). These fixtures are not semantic labels or corpus training examples.

The [first attempt](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/masked-contrastive-01/numerical/failure.json) stopped before numerical work: isolated Python could not see the user-site PyTorch installation. Its failure artifacts and original helper remain unchanged. The separately frozen [native-site launcher](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/assay_masked_contrastive_native.py) selected the existing installation by explicit path insertion, without processing that user site's `.pth` files. It completed under PyTorch 2.13.0+cu130 on CPU, with one compute thread and one interop thread, 0.96 seconds of worker CPU time, 1.00 seconds of measured worker wall time and a peak observed RSS of 571,128 KiB. The two untrained fixture models contain four Linear modules and 370,048 parameter scalars; four forward calls were observed. Parameter/buffer values and the caller's CPU RNG stayed unchanged.

Subsequent review identified that this launcher executes its helper before checking the helper hash. All selected helper bytes matched before and after the actual run; no wrong-helper execution was observed. The original launcher and completed evidence remain frozen. The separate [root verifier](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/validate_masked_contrastive.py) checks the pinned helper and driver bytes independently before compiling those same bytes in its actual native replay child. This supplements the evidence without retroactively qualifying the original loader's ordering. A corrected standalone loader needs a separate immutable version.

The numerical worker is separate from the existing raw-ranking worker, which deliberately forbids optional model stacks. Its report identifies the actual interpreter/PyTorch version, CPU work, measured resident memory, enforced limits and fixture model activity. A CUDA-linked framework can reserve substantial virtual address space without loading an encoder checkpoint. Observed RSS and a cooperative memory gate must not be presented as an OS-enforced address-space sandbox.

Backpropagation through untrained fixture heads is counted separately from optimizer updates and real model inference. No historical embedding is used in the synthetic objective, no semantic training runs, and no encoder, decoder, Leanstral or prover is invoked. Existing native8/native768 projection profiles and an objective-bound checkpoint/reload format remain later work.

## Legacy compatibility and remaining work

The old [alignment projection loss](../../external/ipfs_datasets/ipfs_datasets_py/logic/formalization/autoencoder/alignment_projection.py) remains unchanged. The new objective should match its scalar and representation gradients when every old negative is permitted. It adds exclusion without silently changing the old implementation profile or checkpoint meaning.

Static review also found a limitation in the existing [IR loss configuration](../../external/ipfs_datasets/ipfs_datasets_py/optimizers/logic_theorem_optimizer/legal_ir_loss_configuration.py): `filter_false_negatives` builds its admission lookup from all admitted pair classes, and `_evaluate_normalized_cosine` likewise ignores pair class. Consequently, positive/negative classification needs an explicit migration check before these helpers can supply the new masks. Other lineage/pair exclusions may filter a given candidate, but pair-class admission alone is insufficient. This source-bound finding is not a repaired legacy contract or a measured production failure. The new numerical owner does not import that filter.

The [validation receipt](../../artifacts/autoformalization-alignment-20261003/masked-contrastive-01/validation.json) records 365 passing targeted tests: 53 new [loss tests](../../external/ipfs_datasets/tests/unit/logic/formalization/autoencoder/test_alignment_masked_contrastive.py), 46 new [relation tests](../../external/ipfs_datasets/tests/unit/logic/formalization/autoencoder/test_alignment_relation_declarations.py), 36 existing projection tests and 230 existing lane/stage declaration tests. It records assay replay, lint evidence and the preceding 190 bound files. These checks establish engineering behavior, not source fidelity or retrieval improvement. The previously documented legacy adapter-registry mismatch remains preserved; this slice does not claim a fully passing legacy repository or a complete dependency closure.

Remaining work is authenticated relation admission, evidence-bound tensor mask construction, objective/profile/checkpoint identity, finite-gradient update gates and private reload checks in a separate fit worker. OS-enforced reference confinement and authenticated derivative/split provenance also remain open. Reviewed source fidelity and the broad 40-family pilot still precede claims of improved retrieval quality, formalization accuracy or proof coverage. Leanstral's optional embedding role retains its separate runtime qualification requirements.
