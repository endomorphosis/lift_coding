\begin{abstract}
We study whether native source-linked semantic context improves cold program
repair under fixed resources. Our supervision design records the subject,
scope, and authority of evidence separately from operational completion.
We compare matched public raw context (A) with the same raw context plus native
semantic context (B), using Grok 4.6 across eight historical repository families
and two nested repetitions per arm. All 32 fixed cells reached terminal
accounting after one proposal POST each. Nine yielded useful repairs: A achieved
5/16 and B 4/16. The mean family-level B-minus-A difference was $-0.0625$, with
a frozen descriptive 95\% percentile interval of $[-0.1875,0]$.
Fourteen requests reached the child deadline. Sixteen scoring invocations
included one incomplete hidden-validation import failure. All provider charges
remain unsettled. These results offer no observed advantage for the combined
semantic-context treatment in this small convenience sample. General semantic
correctness, human fidelity, and withdrawn routing, reuse, and publication
benefits remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}
