\subsection*{P.1  Legal, Intent, and Security IR composition}
\noindent
For the main paper's protected-write example, a Legal declaration can express an applicable constraint,\linebreak[4]
an Intent declaration can describe a plan to request approval and write, and a Security declaration\linebreak[4]
can model whether the implementation enforces the check. An event/trace view can then describe\linebreak[4]
observed requests and writes. A shared name is not sufficient to join them: actor, resource, tenant,\linebreak[4]
operation, time, and policy-version mappings must be admitted. [artifact S01,S04,S09]\linebreak[4]
The plan's postcondition is not evidence that it occurred. A permission in an intent or policy view is\linebreak[4]
not a record of execution. A proof that a model enforces a guard is not a legal-applicability decision.\linebreak[4]
The useful common space is therefore a collection of linked typed declarations and evidence records,\linebreak[4]
with explicit bridge obligations, rather than one blended verdict or universal latent representation.\linebreak[4]
A learned view head can recommend examining temporal structure, but the temporal bridge must\linebreak[4]
still specify its clock and scope. A graph can locate a candidate exemption provision, but the source\linebreak[4]
interpretation must establish applicability. A counterexample can reveal a model defect, but a changed\linebreak[4]
compiler must not remove the difficult premise merely to make the theorem provable. These are the\linebreak[4]
practical reasons for keeping retrieval, semantic interpretation, statistical guidance, and checking\linebreak[4]
separate while permitting structured feedback among them.\par
