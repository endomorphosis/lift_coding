\begin{abstract}
Autoformalization for verifiable coding requires a source-grounded interpretation and a computational route for checking it. We describe a compiler-guided architecture that couples engineered symbolic intermediate representations (IRs) to adaptive multi-view representation learning, and we report the automated structural, native-training, and checker evidence that was actually obtained. Pipeline comparisons A--E vary the source-to-proof mechanism. Learning comparisons T0--T5 vary sample memory, shared parameters, isolated proof heads, promoted guidance, and compiler repair. Those two matrices answer different questions and are not merged.

We report automated structural correctness, exact-goal native checker receipts, teacher-agreement diagnostics, learned-update records, and finite constructed conformance. Independent human source-facet fidelity and agreement were not collected. Checker and teacher success are not original-source semantic gold. On the locked 1913-unit final-test population, matched A--E coverage and cost remain unrun, independent fidelity is unmeasured, and native useful-proof transfer is unavailable on the sealed checker \texttt{PATH}. Shared-parameter T2 accepted no sealed-\texttt{PATH} epochs; a T1 seen-source teacher-cosine gap is a sample-memory diagnostic, not generalization. T3 has no eligible native labels; T4 and Arm~E remain unactivated; T5 has no measured held-out improvement. A synthetic policy--code--trace example and twelve reconstructed finite semantic checks illustrate composition and are not natural-distribution accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Autoformalization makes heterogeneous material available to mechanical reasoning, but a formula can be well typed and provable while misrepresenting its source~\citep{wu2022autoformalization,azerbayev2023proofnet,jiang2024multilanguage}. A requirement, an implementation, and an execution trace also have different truth conditions: an obligation is not an observation, permission is not execution, and belief is not fact. Mathematical autoformalization demonstrates useful natural-language translation into formal statements. The coding-verification question is how to learn better source-to-symbolic mappings while preserving those distinctions, without treating retrieval, planning, or a solver candidate as a proof.

The method couples a deterministic semantic pipeline to adaptive representation learning. Compilers create source-grounded IRs, decomposers expose multiple logical views, and decompilers reconstruct inspectable content. An adaptive modal autoencoder learns reusable feature-to-embedding and feature-to-family/view parameters against teacher targets. Reconstruction, structural, provenance, grammar, and cross-view diagnostics identify where the symbolic pipeline is inadequate. Bounded optimization then either updates shared parameters or proposes a separately validated compiler/decompiler change. Checked proof feedback, when admitted, trains isolated prediction heads rather than rewarding arbitrary changes that make the input easier to prove.

This is neither one end-to-end language model nor unsupervised discovery of a universal logic. The schema, operators, sorts, and admissibility rules are engineered. Learning estimates content, associations, rankings, and repair priorities within those contracts. Several implemented paths coexist: deterministic compilation, adaptive diagnostic learning, packed tensor updates, source-withheld reconstruction, and bounded model-assisted synthesis. We distinguish their inputs, outputs, objectives, and activation.

The original draft asked whether shared learned representations and checked feedback improve independently judged source fidelity and useful proof coverage on unseen material. Independent human labels were not collected, so that question remains unmeasured. The measured contribution of this manuscript is narrower:

\begin{enumerate}
\item a source-grounded account of compilation, multi-view IRs, and adaptive learning, with equations audited for reconstruction versus text, source maps versus truth, planning versus proof, and implemented versus executed tense;
\item matched pipeline conditions A--E and learning conditions T0--T5, reported separately, with every primary final-test cell retained as unrun, unavailable, or unmeasured rather than as a silent zero;
\item finite constructed conformance, solver-local policy--code--trace witnesses, development exact-goal checker receipts, and native-training diagnostics, each labeled by population and not promoted to held-out source-semantic gold.
\end{enumerate}

Historical PDF recovery text is retained under \texttt{pages/} as provenance. That recovery copy still contains unmeasured promises; this manuscript does not present those promises as results.

\section{Representations, teachers, and reconstruction}
\subsection{What is shared, and what is learned}
Three axes remain distinct. Source format names prose, code, traces, tables, or extracted media. Domain names Legal, Security, Intent, software, or UI declarations. Logical view names deontic, temporal, frame, first-order, cognitive/event, graph, or proof-oriented structure. Sharing canonical records does not equate domain ontologies or logical interpretations.

The domain-neutral kernel supplies canonical bytes, identities, provenance, source spans, assumptions, claims, obligations, and result protocols. Domain adapters retain their own semantic slots. A symbolic declaration is discrete and inspectable; its content identifier binds an encoding under a declared profile, not a learned location in a vector space. Equal vectors or nearby embeddings do not imply equivalent formulas. Six intermediate objects have different roles: canonical symbolic declarations, family-specific formulas, learned numerical features, graph relationships, search plans, and proof/evidence records. A search plan is not a formula; a declaration is not its verification result; and a vector is not a proof.

\subsection{Supervisory targets are teachers, not source gold}
The concrete legal training record contains source identity, normalized text, an embedding-model identifier and vector, a modal IR document, frame candidates, parser trace, and losses. The default convenience embedding is a deterministic mock (\texttt{mock:stable-sha256}); a local codec can supply spaCy-derived feature hashing; externally supplied vectors carry their producer identity. Modal-family targets can be derived from parsed operators; view targets come from bridge contracts; frames can be retrieval-selected; proof-feedback labels require admitted verifier records. Reproducing these labels measures distillation of those teachers. Independent annotations are required to measure fidelity to the original source, and those annotations were not collected.

For a source $x$, write $I=C_\psi(x)$ for the symbolic compiler output, $e=T(x)$ for the declared embedding target, and $f=F(x,I)$ for reusable features. Features include lexical cues, actor/action/object roles, conditions and exceptions, temporal structure, predicate arity, quantifier scope, provenance, frame relations, compiler contracts, and reconstruction diagnostics. The adaptive model predicts an embedding $\hat e$, a modal-family distribution $p_F$, and a view distribution $p_V$. Some features depend on an existing parse, so this is often parser-assisted learning, not a demonstrated raw-text-only compiler.

\subsection{Compilers and two meanings of reconstruction}
The local modal codec uses deterministic registry cues, spaCy-derived features, BM25 frame grounding, and frame-logic consistency checks. A selected structured-text profile has a seven-field rule---modality, actor, action, object, conditions, exceptions, and temporal qualifiers---and explicitly disables learned guidance and repair. Formalization adapters produce named formula views and source maps. The maps bind IR constituents to source evidence; they are not proof of a correct interpretation. Media extraction is a limitation: no OCR/ASR theorem performance is claimed.

\input{figure-01.tex}

Symbolic reconstruction follows
\begin{equation}
I_1=C_\psi(x),\qquad \hat{x}=D_\omega(I_1),\qquad I_2=C_\psi(\hat{x}).
\label{eq:cycle}
\end{equation}
A decompiler renders roles, predicates, modality, conditions, exceptions, and temporal structure into inspectable text. Source-withheld evaluation gives the realizer the IR, not the originating source, locator, source map, private parser state, or gold target. Comparing $I_1$ and $I_2$ tests reconstructable facets and compiler/realizer consistency. It does not prove source fidelity: both programs could consistently omit the same exception. Forward (gold$\to$first IR), cycle (first IR$\to$second IR), and end-to-end (gold$\to$second IR) scores remain distinct.

The autoencoder's numerical reconstruction is different. Encode builds a state containing family predictions and an embedding projection; decode returns that projection as a numeric vector. Its basic reconstruction target is the sample embedding, not arbitrary source prose. Calling this vector reconstruction a trained free-text decoder would misstate the implementation: numerical decode is not a trained free-text decoder.

\section{Adaptive learning and assistance}
\subsection{Shared parameters versus sample memory}
The adaptive implementation has sparse parameter groups for feature embeddings, modal families, semantic slots, predicate arguments, logic signatures, view logits, decompiler plans, and family--slot--view interactions. It also retains compatibility fields indexed by sample identity. The generalizable training route disables per-sample memory for updates and evaluation. Reconstructing a stored vector for a known sample is not generalization. A schematic of the shared heads is
\begin{equation}
\hat e=e_0(f)+\sum_g A_g(f)W_g,
\label{eq:emb}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
p_F=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\ell^0_F(f)+\sum_g A_g(f)U_g\right),\qquad
p_V=\mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\ell^0_V(f)+\sum_g A_g(f)V_g\right).
\label{eq:logits}
\end{equation}
Here $A_g$ is activity of a bounded feature group, and $W_g,U_g,V_g$ are enabled embedding and logit tables. This notation summarizes the packed implementation; it is not a Transformer or variational autoencoder. A forward-only no-op is reported separately from a parameter update.

\subsection{Packed objective and isolated proof heads}
The packed tensor path gathers active rows, constructs predictions, reduces losses in FP32, backpropagates, clips gradients, applies an SGD-style step, and scatters changed rows into transaction-aware state. The inspected update is not Adam. For the active heads,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
L_{\mathrm{packed}}={}&H(q_F,p_F)+H(q_V,p_V)+\mathrm{MSE}(e,\hat e)\\
&+\lambda_c(1-\cos(e,\hat e))+L_{\mathrm{norm}}+\lambda_2 R(\Theta).
\end{split}
\label{eq:packed}
\end{equation}
Targets are normalized distributions; masks exclude unavailable rows. Neither cosine similarity nor cross-entropy is a theorem about recovered meaning. Appendix~\ref{app:packed} records the globally normalized masked CE and the SGD-style step actually audited.

Seven explicit heads can predict obligation family, semantic slots, premise family, route availability, trusted outcome, reconstruction success, and minimal failing contract. Their declared weight in the primary representation objective is zero. A prediction of proof success can prioritize a route but cannot certify a formula.

Stable-feature promotion is an implemented join, not an executed consumer result in this manuscript. Export excludes raw source, token-level copying channels, sample identifiers, and sample-memory state. Admission requires paired fixed-canary evidence and rollback metadata. File existence or a matching ingestion digest is not applied learned-feature guidance. The frozen canonical compiler profile continues to disable learned guidance.

\subsection{Retrieval, planning, and proof proposals}
BM25, vector indexes, and knowledge-graph traversal supply complementary context rather than truth criteria. Index construction is not encoder training. Context becomes a premise only after grounding, applicability, and policy checks; absence from retrieval is not logical negation.

The Tactician deterministically orders caller-provided sources under an explicit policy and creates an acyclic subgoal plan. Planning performs no proof. The ITP Hammer selects premises, lowers a supported first-order-safe fragment, runs an allowed solver portfolio, and attempts native reconstruction. Solver output is a candidate until reconstruction is accepted by the originating checker. Leanstral supplies advisory proof-engineering proposals; a model response does not replace checking. Generated Lean containing \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, or unapproved axioms is rejected before any kernel claim. These mechanisms are implemented routes. On the sealed validation \texttt{PATH}, native Lean/Z3/CVC5/Vampire/E/Coq/Isabelle assistance was unavailable, and no Hammer or Leanstral benchmark from other papers was run.

\section{Multi-view translation and a coding example}
\subsection{Profile-indexed semantics and a conditional transfer rule}
Each logical view has a signature, sentence language, semantic models, satisfaction relation, and a supported query fragment. For illustration, a normal modality can be interpreted with an indexed accessibility relation
\begin{equation}
[\![\Box_i\phi]\!](w)=\forall v\,[R_i(w,v)\Rightarrow[\![\phi]\!](v)].
\label{eq:modal}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:modal} is an illustrative semantic recipe, not a claim that every repository view is compiled by it. Ordinary normal modal logic is not a universal deontic solution~\citep{benzmuller2020logikey}. $O(Gp)$ and $G(Op)$ are not equated without a profile-specific commutation result.

A translation record must state source/target views, profile, signature map, assumptions, supported constructs, named losses, preserved property, and checker evidence. Translation validation examines the produced transformation rather than trusting every compiler invocation~\citep{pnueli1998translation}. A legacy TDFOL-to-FOL converter that erases unary temporal and deontic operators and replaces strong until by conjunction is a negative control; a resulting solver success is not a valid modal proof.

Let $\Gamma\models_{L_s}\phi$ be the desired source-view consequence. A translator $\tau$ maps premises and goal into $L_t$. Under bridge assumptions $B$, let $R_B(s,t)$ relate admitted source and target models. The required directionality is
\begin{equation}
s\models\Gamma\land R_B(s,t)\;\Rightarrow\; t\models\tau(\Gamma),
\label{eq:prem}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
t\models\tau(\phi)\land R_B(s,t)\;\Rightarrow\; s\models\phi.
\label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
For every admitted source model satisfying $\Gamma$ and $B$ there must be a related target model; otherwise the relation can be vacuous. If a target checker soundly establishes $\tau(\Gamma)\models\tau(\phi)$, these conditions transfer the consequence back. This is a \emph{conditional paper proposition} to discharge per supported translation, not a machine-checked soundness theorem about every implementation translator. Inconsistent premises are refused rather than exploded into an accepted transfer.

\subsection{Synthetic policy--code--trace illustration}
Consider a synthetic coding requirement: ``Protected resources must not be written without approval. Each write must be audited within two steps.'' It is a specification example, not legal advice and not a production experiment. One source supplies the requirement, a second supplies implementation text, and a third supplies observations. The normative view may represent the first sentence as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Protected}(r)\land\neg\mathrm{Approved}(a,r,t)\;\Rightarrow\; O_{a,t,\theta}(\neg\mathrm{Write}(a,r,t)).
\label{eq:obl}
\end{equation}
The policy-to-compliance bridge defines which observed behaviors count as violations. It does not derive actual non-writing from the obligation. The compliance queries are
\begin{equation}
Q_1=\forall a,r,t:\;\mathrm{Protected}(r)\land\neg\mathrm{Approved}(a,r,t)\Rightarrow\neg\mathrm{Write}(a,r,t),
\label{eq:q1}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Q_2=\forall a,r,t:\;\mathrm{Write}(a,r,t)\Rightarrow\exists u\in[t,t+2]:\mathrm{Audit}(a,r,u).
\label{eq:q2}
\end{equation}
A deterministic implementation model $\mathrm{Write}=\neg\mathrm{Protected}\lor\mathrm{Approved}$ makes $Q_1$ hold on that finite Boolean model. An always-write implementation supplies a countermodel when the resource is protected and approval is false. Transferring either result to actual source code requires a justified extraction or refinement relation; finding the word ``approve'' in a function name is insufficient. For $Q_2$, a write at step~0 and a matching audit at step~1 satisfy the bounded requirement for that observed event; an audit at step~3 violates it only when capture is complete through the deadline; an incomplete window yields unknown; a same-looking name in a different tenant cannot be joined without an admitted identity mapping. These are finite constructed witnesses, not compiler soundness and not Table~\ref{tab:pipeline-results}.

\section{Related work}
Mathematical autoformalization with large language models~\citep{wu2022autoformalization,azerbayev2023proofnet,jiang2024multilanguage} motivates learned statement generation, typically into proof-assistant mathematics. Our source pipeline combines domain-specific parsing, structured teacher targets, adaptive representation heads, and checked compilation guidance for policy, code, and trace material. We do not report ProofNet, miniF2F, or multilingual math-autoformalization scores; those benchmarks were not run.

Retrieval-augmented and informal-to-formal proving~\citep{jiang2022thor,jiang2023dsp,yang2023leandojo,first2023baldur} integrate language models with hammers or whole-proof generation. Thor and Sledgehammer~\citep{blanchette2013sledgehammer} reconstruct solver candidates in an originating ITP. Our Hammer and Leanstral routes are the corresponding implemented mechanisms, but sealed-\texttt{PATH} native checkers were unavailable, so we do not claim LeanDojo/Thor/Baldur performance.

Verified coding systems such as CompCert~\citep{leroy2009compcert}, seL4~\citep{klein2009sel4}, and Dafny~\citep{leino2010dafny} supply the workshop's coding-verification context: a specification, an implementation, and a machine-checked artifact. Our synthetic protected-write/audit example is a finite illustration of that use case, not a verified kernel, compiler, or program verifier, and those systems were not re-benchmarked here.

Learned perception plus symbolic execution is established in neural-symbolic reasoning~\citep{yi2018nsvqa}; here provenance, modal scope, and separate proof-feedback objectives are material. We do not claim to originate multi-logic interoperability: institutions formalize signatures, models, and satisfaction~\citep{goguen1992institutions}, while LogiKEy combines normative logics by HOL embedding~\citep{benzmuller2020logikey}. Frame logic, event calculus, and translation validation are established foundations~\citep{kifer1995flogic,kowalski1986events,pnueli1998translation}.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
Independent human semantic-fidelity, agreement, and adjudication were not performed; their protocols remain unexecuted future work and supply no empirical score. Teacher bias, sample-memory shortcuts, lossy legacy translations, bounded backend coverage, and unactivated guidance are retained limitations. Constructor or route availability is distinct from supported semantic fragments and live checker coverage. A CID binds encoding, not interpretation. A trained view classifier, valid source map, or high reconstruction score does not establish faithful interpretation. Result kinds remain distinct: theorem proof, satisfiability, finite runtime observation, evidence readiness, and policy approval.

The methodology is a coupled learning and compilation system: symbolic structure supplies auditable targets; trainable shared representations expose systematic errors; constrained proposals and fixed-canary admission are the intended compiler-facing path; modality-aware views support explicitly justified reasoning. What this manuscript establishes is that inventory: implemented A--E and T0--T5 conditions, honest unrun/unavailable/unmeasured primary cells on 1913 locked final-test units, a supported T1 memorization diagnostic, an unsupported sealed-\texttt{PATH} T2 shared-learner update, constructed conformance and policy--code--trace witnesses, and development checker receipts that are not source gold. It does not establish that the cycle improves independent source fidelity or useful cross-view proof coverage on genuinely unseen material.

\section{Packed training equations}
\label{app:packed}
The packed reconstruction term averages over embedding coordinates,
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{rec}}=\frac1n\sum_i\frac1d\|\hat e_i-e_i\|_2^2,
\label{eq:rec}
\end{equation}
and masked family/view cross-entropy is globally normalized over eligible rows $M$,
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{CE}}=-\frac1n\sum_{i\in M}\sum_j q_{ij}\log\mathrm{softmax}(\ell_i)_j.
\label{eq:ce}
\end{equation}
The inspected update is SGD-style,
\begin{equation}
\Theta\leftarrow\Theta-\eta\,\mathrm{Clip}(\nabla_\Theta L).
\label{eq:sgd}
\end{equation}
These identities describe the audited packed-CPU path. They are not a claim that every configured head was enabled in a deployed checkpoint, and they are not held-out source fidelity.

\section{Conditional proof-transfer proposition}
\label{app:prop}
\paragraph{Proposition 1 (conditional semantic consequence transfer).}
\label{prop:transfer}
Assume a translator $\tau$, bridge assumptions $B$, and a relation $R_B$ that is nonvacuous on the admitted source models of $\Gamma$. If Equations~\ref{eq:prem}--\ref{eq:goal} hold, premises are consistent in the relevant sense, and a sound target checker establishes $\tau(\Gamma)\models\tau(\phi)$, then $\Gamma\models_{L_s}\phi$.
This is a paper-level conditional argument, not a theorem that every repository translator satisfies. Related-model existence, premise preservation, goal reflection, and checker identity must be discharged per supported fragment. The legacy TDFOL converter does not satisfy the contract.
