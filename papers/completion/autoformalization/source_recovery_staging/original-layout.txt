                                                         Abstract

 1             Autoformalization requires both faithful interpretation and a computational route
 2             for reasoning about it. We describe a compiler-guided architecture coupling en-
 3             gineered symbolic IRs to adaptive multi-view representation learning. Determin-
 4             istic parsers construct source-grounded declarations; decompilers expose source-
 5             withheld reconstruction errors; and shared feature, family, semantic-slot, and view
 6             parameters are optimized by an implemented packed tensor update and guarded
 7             outer selection. BM25, vector indexes, and knowledge-graph traversal supply
 8             complementary context rather than truth criteria. A policy-constrained Tactician
 9             organizes search, an ITP Hammer translates supported fragments and reconstructs
10             solver candidates, and Leanstral supplies advisory proof-engineering proposals. In-
11             dependently admitted feedback trains isolated proof heads or motivates separately
12             tested compiler/decompiler changes. Stable learned guidance reaches enabled
13             consumers only through source-free, fixed-canary promotion. Shared symbols
14             and explicit preservation contracts connect deontic, temporal, frame, event, graph,
15             Legal, Security, and Intent views without equating their semantics. We distinguish
16             numerical reconstruction from symbolic round trips, and model prediction from
17             checked proof. A synthetic policy–code–trace example and twelve standalone
18             semantic checks illustrate composition. This source-audited methods draft reports
19             no new checkpoint training or end-to-end performance: held-out source fidelity,
20             retrieval benefit, native proof coverage, and total learning/verification cost remain
21             to be measured.


22   1    Introduction
23   Autoformalization makes heterogeneous material available to mechanical reasoning, but a formula
24   can be well typed and provable while misrepresenting its source. A requirement, implementation, and
25   execution trace also have different truth conditions: an obligation is not an observation, permission
26   is not execution, and belief is not fact. Recent mathematical autoformalization demonstrates useful
27   natural-language translation into formal statements [1, 2, 9]. Our question is how to learn better
28   source-to-symbolic mappings while preserving these distinctions across domains and logical views.
29   The method couples a deterministic semantic pipeline to adaptive representation learning. Compilers
30   create source-grounded intermediate representations (IRs), decomposers expose multiple logical
31   views, and decompilers reconstruct inspectable content. An adaptive modal autoencoder learns
32   reusable feature-to-embedding and feature-to-family/view parameters against these targets. Recon-
33   struction, structural, provenance, grammar, and cross-view diagnostics identify where the symbolic
34   pipeline is inadequate. Bounded optimization then either updates model parameters or proposes a
35   separately validated compiler/decompiler improvement. Checked proof feedback supplies additional,
36   isolated prediction heads rather than rewarding arbitrary changes that make the input easier to prove.

38   The schema, operators, sorts, and admissibility rules are engineered. Learning estimates content,
39   associations, rankings, and repair priorities within those contracts. Several paths coexist: determin-
40   istic compilation, adaptive diagnostic learning, genuine tensor gradient updates, source-withheld
41   reconstruction, and bounded model-assisted synthesis. We distinguish their inputs, outputs, objectives,
42   and activation instead of describing all of them as one autoencoder.
43   The contribution of this methods draft is: (i) a source-grounded account of representation learn-
44   ing and compiler/decompiler improvement; (ii) an explicit account of BM25, vector, and graph
45   retrieval, policy-constrained proof planning, Hammer reconstruction, and Leanstral guidance; and
46   (iii) composition of complementary IR views through typed identities and justified translations. A
47   worked policy–code–trace example and finite semantic witnesses illustrate the necessary boundaries.
48   The final learned-fidelity, transfer, checkpoint, and cost experiments remain marked for completion.
49   Source inspection establishes implementation mechanisms, not successful training of every optional
50   configuration.

51   2     Representations and training examples
52   2.1   What is shared, and what is learned?

53   Three axes must remain distinct. Source format names prose, code, traces, tables, or extracted media.
54   Domain names Legal, Security, Intent, software, or UI declarations. Logical view names deontic,
55   temporal, frame, first-order, cognitive/event, graph, or proof-oriented structure. Sharing canonical
56   records does not equate domain ontologies or logical interpretations. [artifact S01–S06]
57   The domain-neutral kernel supplies canonical bytes, identities, provenance, source spans, assumptions,
58   claims, obligations, and result protocols. Domain adapters retain their own semantic slots. The syntax
59   kernel supplies typed terms, binders, signatures, connectives, and versioned family extensions. A
60   symbolic declaration is therefore discrete and inspectable; its CID identifies an encoding under a
61   declared profile, not a learned location in a vector space. Equal vectors or nearby embeddings do not
62   imply equivalent formulas. [artifact S01,S05]
63   Six intermediate objects have different roles: canonical symbolic declarations, family-specific
64   formulas, learned numerical features, graph relationships, search plans, and proof/evidence records.
65   A search plan is not a formula; a declaration is not its verification result; and a vector is not a proof.
66   Their interchange requires explicit mappings. A model can select or repair a candidate without
67   defining its semantics. [artifact S01,S04,S34,S38]

68   2.2   How examples and supervisory targets are constructed

69   The concrete legal training record contains source identity and citation, raw and normalized text,
70   an embedding-model identifier and vector, a modal IR document, frame candidates and selection,
71   parser trace, and losses. A builder normalizes a U.S.-Code-style passage, parses modal formulas,
72   ranks ontology frames using BM25, and packages the result. The default convenience embedding is
73   explicitly a deterministic mock; the local codec can instead supply spaCy-derived feature hashing,
74   and externally supplied vectors carry their producer identity. Experiments must record which path
75   supplied the target vector. [artifact S22,S23]
76   Thus targets are not all human gold labels. Modal-family targets can be derived from parsed operators;
77   view targets come from bridge contracts and their configured scoring; frames can be retrieval-selected;
78   and proof-feedback labels require admitted verifier records. Reproducing these labels measures
79   distillation of those teachers. Independent annotations and held-out semantic distinctions are required
80   to measure fidelity to the original source.
81   For a source x, write I = Cψ (x) for the symbolic compiler output, e = T (x) for the declared embed-
82   ding target, and f = F (x, I) for reusable features. Features include lexical cues, actor/action/object
83   roles, conditions and exceptions, temporal structure, predicate arity, quantifier scope, provenance,
84   frame relations, compiler contracts, and reconstruction diagnostics. The adaptive model predicts an
85   embedding ê, a modal-family distribution pF , and a view distribution pV . Some features depend on
86   an existing parse, so this is often parser-assisted learning, not a demonstrated raw-text-only universal
87   compiler. [artifact S24]

88    3     Compilers, decompilers, and reconstruction loops

89    3.1   The deterministic source-to-symbolic path

90    The local modal codec uses deterministic registry cues, spaCy-derived features, BM25 frame ground-
91    ing, and frame-logic consistency checks. It produces a structured modal declaration before optional
92    expensive model repair. A separate selected structured-text profile has a narrower seven-field rule:
93    modality, actor, action, object, conditions, exceptions, and temporal qualifiers. Its modality vo-
94    cabulary is obligation, permission, and prohibition; its atom vocabulary is supplied by the caller.
95    Unsupported semantics trigger abstention or disclosed partial output. The selected profile explicitly
96    disables learned guidance and repair: training elsewhere in the repository does not imply that this
97    frozen profile silently uses it. [artifact S07,S08,S23,S25]
98    Formalization adapters produce named formula views and source maps. The maps bind IR constituents
99    to source evidence; they are not proof of a correct interpretation. Source-code extraction offers
100   additional candidate security facts, with explicit heuristic and coverage limitations. Media requires a
101   separately measured extraction layer before this logic pipeline; no end-to-end audio/image theorem
102   performance is established here. [artifact S09,S13–S16]

103   3.2   Two different meanings of reconstruction

104   Symbolic reconstruction follows
                                I1 = Cψ (x),       x̂ = Dω (I1 ),     I2 = Cψ (x̂).                        (1)
105   A decompiler renders roles, predicates, modality, conditions, exceptions, and temporal structure
106   into inspectable text. The canonical source-withheld evaluation gives the realizer the IR, not the
107   originating source, its locator, source map, private parser state, or gold target. Comparing I1 and I2
108   tests reconstructable facets and compiler/realizer consistency. It does not prove source fidelity: both
109   programs could consistently omit the same exception. [artifact S07]
110   The adaptive autoencoder’s numerical reconstruction is different. Its encode method builds a state
111   containing family predictions and an embedding projection; its decode returns that projection as
112   a numeric vector. Its basic reconstruction target is the sample embedding, not arbitrary source
113   prose. Textual decompilation and structural cycle metrics enter through the codec and multi-view
114   bridge. Calling this vector reconstruction a trained free-text decoder would misstate the inspected
115   implementation. [artifact S24]

116   3.3   Constrained candidates and transfer beyond the legal teacher

117   The constrained decoder gives learned heads a closed production space: valid-token, grammar, binder,
118   type, and family masks precede candidate scoring. The inspected profile declares a frozen 143-token
119   vocabulary and bounded search, but these declarations are not a learned tokenizer checkpoint or
120   evidence of end-to-end sequence training. The learner may rank admitted productions; a deterministic
121   grammar decides what can be represented. [artifact S33]
122   The original sample builder is legal-specific. A domain-neutral formalization/advisor layer permits
123   other domain adapters to share feature, view, checkpoint, and result contracts, but it does not
124   automatically turn a legal-trained model into a calibrated Security or Intent formalizer. Cross-domain
125   use needs explicit adapter mappings and separate evaluation. Shared infrastructure, statistical transfer,
126   and semantic proof transfer are three distinct claims. [artifact S01,S17,S22]

140   4.2   Actual gradient objectives

141   The packed tensor implementation gathers active rows, constructs predictions, reduces losses in
142   FP32, backpropagates, clips gradients, applies an SGD-style step, and scatters changed rows into
143   transaction-aware state. BF16 activations are optional after a numerical check; the same packed
144   path has a CPU reference. This is real differentiation through tensor losses, not just the word “SGD”
145   applied to a prose critique loop. The inspected update is not an Adam update. [artifact S26–S28]
146   For the active heads, its objective is
                              Lpacked = H(qF , pF ) + H(qV , pV ) + MSE(e, ê)
                                        + λc (1 − cos(e, ê)) + Lnorm + λ2 R(Θ).                          (5)
147   Targets are normalized distributions; masks exclude unavailable target rows. Reconstruction is
148   averaged over embedding coordinates, cosine loss guards zero-norm vectors, the auxiliary term
149   penalizes extreme norm ratios, and regularization is normalized over parameters. Terms for inactive
150   update targets are zero. The outer controller additionally scores structural/view losses and regression
151   constraints; it is not simply Equation 5 applied to every available metric. [artifact S27]

152   Family targets may be mixed. Evaluation reports cross-entropy, target entropy, and excess cross-
153   entropy. Since H(q, p) − H(q) = DKL (q∥p), a nonzero cross-entropy need not be avoidable error:
154   it can include the target distribution’s own entropy. Neither cosine similarity nor cross-entropy is a
155   theorem about recovered meaning. [artifact S24]

156   4.3   Guarded optimization rather than an unrestricted fit

157   The generalizable projection trainer selects update groups—family logits, decoded embeddings,
158   global or feature-specific view logits, and combined updates—and evaluates candidate learning-rate
159   steps. It can concentrate on hard examples, use bounded pre-screening, and select a candidate only
160   when the chosen objective improves without disallowed regression. Candidate changes are evaluated
161   transactionally and rolled back before selection; timeout, no-improvement, and regression remain
162   visible. A configurable deadband can relax a named numerical tolerance, so its mode and protected
163   metrics belong in the experiment manifest. [artifact S24]
164   Validation examples supplied to this repeated selection procedure are model-selection data, not an
165   untouched test set. Moreover, when no validation set is supplied, the inspected function falls back
166   to evaluating training samples. The paper’s generalization claims therefore require explicit disjoint
167   source-family splits, separate final test data, disabled sample memory, and frozen targets. Source
168   derivatives and multiple views of one passage must not cross partitions.

169   4.4   Proof-aware auxiliary supervision

170   Seven explicit heads predict obligation family, semantic slots, premise family, route availability,
171   trusted outcome, reconstruction success, and minimal failing contract. Categorical heads use nor-
172   malized class probabilities; multilabel heads use independent sigmoid probabilities. Only version-
173   matched, trusted, eligible feedback trains them; duplicates and excluded holdout records are tracked.
174   The code reports coverage, abstention, and family-specific metrics. [artifact S24]
175   Critically, these proof-head parameters are isolated. Their declared weight in the primary representa-
176   tion objective is zero, and protected compiler cross-entropy, embedding, structural reconstruction,
177   provenance, and anti-copy parameter fingerprints are checked before and after an update. A prediction
178   of proof success can prioritize a route but cannot certify a formula. This protects against learning to
179   replace a difficult source statement with an easier, different theorem. [artifact S24]

180   4.5   How learning improves deterministic formalization

181   Introspection exposes feature contributions, embedding residuals, family margins, underrepresented
182   view components, and pipeline-stage diagnostics. The TODO supervisor separates parameter updates
183   from deterministic program-synthesis tasks. A high reconstruction loss can request a shared-weight
184   update; a missing exception-scoping rule requires a compiler/decompiler repair and new regression
185   tests. The cost-gated loop avoids expensive model repair unless it is enabled and its configured
186   expected benefit exceeds call cost. Those estimates are decisions to evaluate, not measured economic
187   guarantees. [artifact S24,S25,S29]
188   Stable-feature promotion is an explicit join between the two loops. The export excludes raw source,
189   token-level copying channels, sample identifiers, and sample-memory state. Integration resolves it
190   against canonical view contracts and requires paired, fixed-canary evidence, including source-copy
191   and symbolic-validity guardrails, before emitting content-addressed guidance with rollback metadata.
192   This is a guarded transfer of learned regularities into a deterministic compiler-facing interface, not
193   gradient descent through arbitrary parser code and not unconstrained rule invention. [artifact S30]

194   5     Retrieval, tacticians, and proof reconstruction
195   5.1   Three retrieval signals, not three truth criteria

196   BM25 supplies lexical candidates: the sample builder ranks frames, while a separate GraphRAG thin
197   client reads sharded postings and applies term-frequency, inverse-frequency, and length-normalization
198   scoring. Vector indexes instead retrieve neighbors of a declared embedding; the inspected FAISS
199   store maintains indexes, metadata, and identifier mappings. Graph adjacency and bounded walks


                                                        5
200   expand typed relationships and dependencies. These signals answer different questions—matching
201   terms, numerical similarity, and explicit connectivity—and none establishes entailment. [artifact
202   S22,S34,S35]
203   Index construction is not encoder training. Nor does a common result envelope establish that every
204   entry point supports every mode: the inspected thin-client CLI declares a vector option but does not
205   dispatch it to an implemented vector query. The actual producer, backend, revision, and route must
206   be recorded. Context found by any index becomes a premise only after grounding, applicability, and
207   policy checks; absence from retrieval is not logical negation. Appendix N details these boundaries.

208   5.2   The Tactician plans; the Hammer searches and reconstructs

209   The repository’s LogicTactician deterministically orders caller-provided sources under an explicit
210   policy, records selected/excluded routes, creates an acyclic subgoal plan, and reports stop or abstention
211   conditions. Planning performs no proof, network work, or writes. Optional model guidance can
212   reorder or nominate admitted sources under a pinned digest, with deterministic fallback. This planner
213   is not itself a trained tactic synthesizer. [artifact S38]
214   The ITP Hammer selects premises, lowers a supported first-order-safe fragment to TPTP or SMT-
215   LIB, runs an allowed bounded solver portfolio, and attempts native reconstruction. Vampire/E are
216   first-order automation routes; Z3/cvc5 are SMT routes; Lean, Coq/Rocq, and Isabelle/HOL have
217   separate frontend/reconstruction contracts. A logic family specifies what a statement means; a prover
218   family specifies a computation over an admitted encoding. Unsupported dependent, higher-order, or
219   unresolved polymorphic constructs cannot be silently erased. External execution is opt-in, and native
220   availability must be established. [artifact S37]
221   Within this Hammer contract, solver output is a candidate until reconstruction is accepted by the
222   originating ITP’s checker in its pinned environment. The deterministic premise baseline uses symbol,
223   type, import, and graph overlap, not a universal vector ranker. Its optional graph-selector artifact uses
224   hand-authored linear weights; the inspected release does not ship a genuinely trained premise-selector
225   checkpoint. Ranking machinery, training a ranker, and proving a goal are separate claims. [artifact
226   S36,S37]

227   5.3   Leanstral proposals and verified training feedback

228   Leanstral supplies advisory proof-engineering candidates, such as Lean edits or strategies. The
229   security lane requires the suggested proof to pass the Lean checker and rejects proof bypasses; a
230   model response does not replace source, runtime, or release evidence. The legal rollout separately
231   admits structured guidance through syntax, provenance, anti-copy, proof, and configured Lean-
232   reconstruction checks. Raw drafts never become reconstruction targets or trusted training labels.
233   Verification creates a new structured artifact rather than making the original prose authoritative.
234   [artifact S39,S40]
235   Together, retrieval nominates context, the Tactician organizes bounded work, Hammer and Leanstral
236   provide different proposal mechanisms, and checking determines the supported result. Admitted feed-
237   back can train isolated heads or identify a compiler defect; a versioned patch still needs independent
238   regression tests and rollout approval. This is not evidence that Leanstral’s base weights are fine-tuned
239   by the adaptive autoencoder. The concrete contracts and return path are given in Appendix O.

240   6     How different IRs are used together
241   6.1   Semantics are indexed by a profile

242   Each logical view has a signature, sentence language, semantic models, satisfaction relation, and a
243   supported query fragment. The common record layer does not force one implementation of these
244   components. A computable representation is not a universal decision procedure: queries may require
245   decidable fragments, bounded search, or explicit unknown/timeout outcomes. First-order and frame
246   views express objects and relations; event-calculus views express events, fluents, and change [6];
247   deontic views express normative force; temporal views bind trace/clock semantics; cognitive views
248   distinguish agent-indexed knowledge, belief, and intention. World, agent, time, action, and formula
249   categories cannot be exchanged merely because their identifiers are strings.


                                                         6
250   For illustration, a normal modality can be interpreted with an indexed accessibility relation:
                                      [[□i ϕ]](w) = ∀v [Ri (w, v) ⇒ [[ϕ]](v)].                              (6)
251   Different Ri and frame axioms express different profiles. A reflexive epistemic relation supports
252   factivity; a serial belief relation need not. A deontic ideal-world relation need not make the obligation
253   true in the actual world. This is an illustrative semantic recipe, not a claim that every repository
254   view is already compiled by Equation 6. Conditional norms, priorities, defeasible exceptions, and
255   contrary-to-duty obligations require their own explicit semantics; ordinary normal modal logic is not
256   a universal deontic solution. LogiKEy provides established semantic embeddings of deontic logics
257   and combinations in HOL [3].

258   The repository’s principal view families. TDFOL explicitly combines first-order, temporal, and
259   deontic syntax; DCEC adds agent-indexed cognitive operators and event-oriented structure. Their
260   source modules expose these constructors, but constructor availability is distinct from complete
261   backend coverage. [artifact S20–S21] The same interpreted norm may yield a deontic force/scope
262   view, frame-based roles, temporal constraints, event/fluent relations, graph traversal edges, and a
263   supported prover encoding. These are complementary task views. A predicted view distribution
264   estimates which interfaces deserve attention; it is not a mixture of truth values. The shared symbol
265   and provenance records permit joins, while each view retains its own semantic profile. [artifact
266   S09,S31]
267   Bare operator letters are unsafe identifiers: F can mean prohibition or eventually, and G can denote a
268   cognitive goal or a temporal always operator. Family, profile, operator kind, arguments, and nesting
269   must survive serialization. In particular, O(Gp) and G(Op) are not equated without a profile-specific
270   commutation result.

271   6.2   Loss-aware translation

272   A translation record must state the source/target views, profile, signature map, assumptions, supported
273   constructs, named losses, preserved property, and checker evidence. Preserving satisfiability is
274   different from preserving the truth of an arbitrary query; one-way abstraction is different from
275   equivalence. Translation validation examines the produced transformation rather than trusting every
276   invocation of a compiler [7].
277   A concrete legacy path makes the need visible: the inspected TDFOL-to-FOL converter removes
278   unary temporal and deontic operators and replaces a binary temporal formula by conjunction. This
279   may serve as a lossy projection, but it cannot generally carry a modal theorem. The paper does not
280   attribute this behavior to every adapter. For example, with p false in the actual world and true in every
281   ideal world, O(p) holds while p does not. On the trace p = [true, false], p holds now but Gp does
282   not. For strong until, q true now makes p U q true regardless of p now, contradicting the proposed
283   replacement p ∧ q. [artifact S18]
284   Encoding a proposition as a predicate such as obligatory(p) also does not supply its semantics. The
285   representation must distinguish quotation/reification from applying a predicate to a term; relational or
286   higher-order embeddings must interpret the modality, not simply preserve its spelling.

287   6.3   A conditional proof-transfer rule

288   Let Γ |=Ls ϕ be the desired source-view consequence. A translator τ maps the premises and goal
289   into Lt . Under bridge assumptions B, let RB (s, t) relate admitted source and target models. The
290   required directionality is:
                                           s |= Γ ∧ RB (s, t) ⇒ t |= τ (Γ),                                 (7)
                                       t |= τ (ϕ) ∧ RB (s, t) ⇒ s |= ϕ.                                     (8)
291   For every admitted source model satisfying Γ and B, there must be a related target model; otherwise
292   the relation can be vacuous. If the target checker soundly establishes τ (Γ) |= τ (ϕ), these conditions
293   transfer the consequence back. A proof sketch appears in Appendix B. This is a contract to discharge
294   per supported translation, not a soundness theorem about every current extractor.
295   Cross-source composition introduces further obligations: entity identities must align; timestamps
296   and units must agree; domain assumptions and exception priorities must be compatible; and the joint


                                                         7
297   premise set must be consistent in the relevant sense. Under classical logic, inconsistent premises
298   entail any conclusion. A system that silently adds an assumption to close a proof has changed the
299   problem rather than solved it.

300   7   Worked example: learned guidance and cross-view reasoning
301   Consider a synthetic coding requirement: “Protected resources must not be written without approval.
302   Each write must be audited within two steps.” It is a specification example, not legal advice or a
303   production experiment. One source supplies the requirement, a second supplies implementation text,
304   and a third supplies observations. Let a be an actor, r a resource, and t a discrete trace position.
305   The normative view may represent the first sentence as:
                        Protected(r) ∧ ¬ Approved(a, r, t) ⇒ Oa,t,θ (¬ Write(a, r, t)).                   (9)
306   The policy-to-compliance bridge defines which observed behaviors count as violations. It does not
307   derive actual non-writing from the obligation. The compliance queries are:
                   Q1 = ∀a, r, t : Protected(r) ∧ ¬ Approved(a, r, t) ⇒ ¬ Write(a, r, t),                (10)
                   Q2 = ∀a, r, t : Write(a, r, t) ⇒ ∃u ∈ [t, t + 2] : Audit(a, r, u).                    (11)
308   A deterministic implementation model sets Write = ¬ Protected ∨ Approved. Substitution estab-
309   lishes Equation 10 in that model. An implementation that always writes supplies a counterexample
310   when the resource is protected and approval is false. Transferring either result to actual source code
311   requires a justified extraction or refinement relation; finding the word “approve” in a function name is
312   insufficient.
313   For Equation 11, an observed write at step 0 and a matching audit at step 1 satisfy the bounded
314   requirement for that event. A matching audit at step 3 violates the two-step requirement only when the
315   capture is complete through the relevant deadline and no earlier matching audit exists. An incomplete
316   window yields unknown, not compliance and not proven violation. The same-looking name in a
317   different tenant or request cannot be joined without an admitted identity mapping. A global program
318   guarantee additionally requires reasoning over all modeled executions, not one successful log.
319   The shared space reuses typed actor/resource/event identities, source maps, and explicit bridge
320   assumptions. A Legal declaration can state the constraint, an Intent declaration can describe the
321   proposed workflow, and a Security declaration can supply implementation properties. The intent
322   is not an observed execution: explicit identity, action, time, and refinement mappings join these
323   views. The resulting behavioral theorem, trace observation, and policy-applicability decision remain
324   different conclusions. [artifact S01,S09]
325   During training, paired variants of this example can change “must” to “may”, move the exception,
326   or change the audit window. The compiler and view metrics provide candidate targets; independent
327   annotations determine whether the intended change survived. A family/view head may identify a
328   temporal omission, a decompiler-plan feature may localize the missing phrase, and a proof-feedback
329   head may rank a useful obligation. None substitutes for the actual current-profile check. This
330   example illustrates how the representations cooperate; it is not reported as an executed learned-model
331   experiment.

332   8   Evidence and evaluation plan
333   This revision audits source code at one frozen repository snapshot. It does not execute the full
334   repository, train a new checkpoint, establish a live CUDA speedup, or show that every supported
335   head is enabled in the selected deployment. Existing CUDA tests check real gradient work, CPU
336   parity, clipping, and resident parameters, but they are skipped without suitable hardware and use
337   convenience fixtures. Their presence is not a new passing test run. [artifact S32]
338   The earlier standalone reference suite remains in the supplement: all twelve finite semantic checks met
339   their expected outcomes, including 26 disagreements between strong-until and conjunction among
340   64 three-step trace pairs. These are illustrations, not accuracy measurements for the repository’s
341   compiler or autoencoder. No training gain is inferred from them.

356   9     Related work, limitations, and conclusion
357   Mathematical autoformalization [1, 2, 9] motivates learned statement generation, but our source
358   pipeline combines domain-specific parsing, structured targets, adaptive representation heads, and
359   checked compilation guidance. Learned perception plus symbolic execution is established in neural-
360   symbolic reasoning [8]; here provenance, modal scope, and separate proof-feedback objectives are
361   material. We do not claim to originate multi-logic interoperability: institutions formalize signatures,
362   models, and satisfaction [4], while LogiKEy combines normative logics by HOL embedding [3].
363   Frame logic, event calculus, and hammer reconstruction are established foundations [5, 6, 10].
364   The main limitations are teacher bias, possible sample-memory shortcuts, repeated validation-set
365   selection, unmeasured checkpoint activation, dynamic source behavior, lossy legacy translations,
366   and bounded backend coverage. The proof space is neither a universal latent space nor a universal
367   decision procedure. A trained view classifier, valid source map, or high reconstruction score does not
368   by itself establish faithful interpretation. Result kinds remain distinct: theorem proof, satisfiability,
369   finite runtime observation, evidence readiness, and policy approval. [artifact S19]
370   The methodology is best understood as a coupled learning and compilation system: symbolic structure
371   supplies auditable targets; trainable shared representations expose systematic errors; constrained
372   proposals and fixed-canary admission improve the compiler-facing path; and modality-aware views
373   support explicitly justified reasoning. The next result to establish is whether this cycle improves
374   independent source fidelity and useful cross-view proof coverage on genuinely unseen material, at a
375   favorable total cost.

417   B     Preservation contract and proof-transfer argument
418   Let SB be the source models admitted under explicit bridge assumptions B. Let τ be fixed on a
419   specified premise and goal language. The contract is not required to cover every source sentence.
420   Proposition 1 (Conditional semantic consequence transfer). Suppose for every s ∈ SB satisfying
421   Γ, there exists a target model t such that RB (s, t) holds. Suppose related models preserve premises
422   in the direction of Equation 7 and reflect the goal in the direction of Equation 8. If a sound target
423   checker establishes τ (Γ) |= τ (ϕ), then Γ |=SB ϕ.

424   Proof. Take any s ∈ SB satisfying Γ. Existence supplies a related t. Premise preservation gives
425   t |= τ (Γ). Target consequence gives t |= τ (ϕ). Goal reflection gives s |= ϕ. The argument holds for
426   each such s.

427   This is a paper-level conditional argument, not a machine-checked theorem of the repository. It
428   does not establish that the source extraction was faithful, that B was appropriate, that the model
429   class was nonempty, or that a legacy converter satisfies the two preservation directions. Test consis-
430   tency/reachability separately to avoid reporting a vacuous result as practical assurance.

506   This is a composed method specification, not a claim that one existing function already performs
507   every step.

508          1. Bind a query to its source references, target property, ontology, allowed assumptions, and
509             acceptance profile.

511             interpretations.
512          3. Elaborate each formula against its signature; preserve modality, scope, time, exception, and
513             source lineage.

516             abstention or explicit partial analysis.
517          6. Check compatibility and nonvacuity of the selected premises; create property-preservation
518             obligations for every relied-upon translation.
519          7. Invoke an available solver or prover under exact theory, environment, and resource bounds.
520             Keep candidate, satisfiability, monitor, and kernel-checked outcomes distinct.
521          8. Reconstruct/check evidence at the assurance level required for the query. Bind the actual
522             verified statement to the outer artifact fields.
523          9. Return the conclusion with all assumptions, sources, bridge records, limitations, and result
524             kind. Unknown does not become true or false by default.
525         10. On counterexamples, distinguish a bad source interpretation, an inadequate abstraction, an

597   J.2   The outer model-selection step

598   The generalizable trainer evaluates family, reconstructed-embedding, global-view, detailed-view,
599   and combined update candidates. It tries learning-rate multipliers, may select hard examples, can
600   cap target construction and pre-screen candidates, and compares candidate objective changes with
601   per-metric regression limits. It temporarily applies candidate state, evaluates, and rolls it back before
602   deciding which candidate to commit. The evaluated objective also includes view/structural losses,
603   which need not be differentiable tensors.
604   This is an inner numerical update surrounded by guarded discrete selection. Calling every stage
605   backpropagation would be wrong. Equally, describing the tensor path as only heuristic tweaking
606   would miss its actual backward and parameter-update operations. If validation samples are absent,
607   evaluation falls back to training examples; that case must not be labeled held-out validation. A
608   nonzero deadband changes the acceptance policy and must be disclosed, not buried as a numerical
609   implementation detail.

610   J.3   Proof-feedback heads and objective isolation

611   The categorical heads are obligation family, trusted outcome, and reconstruction success. The
612   multilabel heads are semantic slots, premise family, proof-route availability, and minimal failing
613   contract. The default vocabulary and family-context sizes are bounded. A head can abstain when its
614   confidence or available labels are inadequate.
615   Training accepts only records passing trust, partition, and compiler/toolchain version checks. Dupli-
616   cate IDs are not trained repeatedly. The first admissible version may pin previously empty proof-head
617   state; mismatches are excluded. The implementation compares protected objective fingerprints and
618   reports a proof-loss weight of zero in the primary representation objective. This is a useful causal
619   design: proof feedback can teach prediction and route selection without being permitted to rewrite
620   the statement solely to maximize provability. Actual downstream benefit still needs an ablation.

621   K     Constrained decoding and learned-to-symbolic promotion
622   K.1   Grammar controls the candidate language

623   The decoder implementation declares typed productions and a frozen vocabulary of 143 tokens,
624   including special, binder, operator, type, family, proof, tactic, source, identifier and production
625   categories. Its declared masks are valid token, grammar, binder, type and family; its search bounds
626   are 16 beam entries and 64 decode steps. These are implementation constants, not results of a newly
627   trained model. The exact profile and any changes belong in the run manifest.

628   This provides an engineered output language in which a model may rank candidates. It prevents
629   impossible JSON shapes or invalid family/type combinations from receiving metric credit. It does
630   not guarantee correct grounding, truth, faithful negation or exception scope. A fallback involving an
631   admitted reference or gold artifact is not an independent blind prediction and must be excluded or
632   reported separately in that evaluation condition.

633   K.2    Feature export is not source copying

634   The stable learned export restricts outputs to reusable feature groups such as compiler contracts,
635   cycle consistency, decompiler surface templates, logic-view contracts, repair lanes and semantic
636   slots. The inspected promotion code explicitly rejects raw-source, source-span, sample-memory, and
637   token-copying markers. It resolves the export against registered contracts and checks fixed-sample
638   paired metrics. Missing guardrail evidence, source-copy regression, symbolic-validity regression or
639   an unfixed sample set blocks guidance emission.
640   The product is a content-addressed guidance record: target contract and family, bounded features,
641   repair suggestions, canary evidence, source-export identity and rollback metadata. Admission of
642   this record is not itself a proof that a specific consumer enabled it. For an end-to-end claim, record
643   the loaded guidance identity and show that the actual compiler or supervisor consumed it under the
644   evaluated configuration. A branch containing training code and a canonical compiler configured with
645   learning disabled are not one measured learned pipeline.

646   K.3    Program synthesis changes the algorithm, not just the weights

647   Introspection compares predicted and target families/views, decoded and target embeddings, semantic-
648   slot diagnostics, source reconstruction, and feature-group ablations. The TODO mechanism distin-
649   guishes learned-parameter actions from deterministic program-synthesis actions. For example, loss
650   caused by poor shared family ranking can lead to a parameter update; loss caused by an unhandled
651   exception pattern can lead to a parser/realizer patch proposal. Such a patch must be checked against
652   preserved source facets and regression cases before its behavior becomes canonical.
653   The local modal TODO supervisor and the broader external agent supervisor are not assumed identical.
654   The paper’s artifact must identify the actual entry point, generated task/proposal, implementation
655   executor, validation record, and activation path used in the experiment. No new autonomous execution
656   is launched by this manuscript.

705   The FAISS store maintains numerical indexes, per-collection metadata, and mappings between

708   query. An evaluated route must identify the actual backend and status. [artifact S35]
709   Three operations must be recorded separately: producing a vector with a frozen or trained encoder;
710   fitting any index structures that a selected index type requires; and changing semantic model parame-

713   interpreted, and a store over vectors does not establish that those vectors came from this autoencoder.

716   verified entry point rather than to that declaration. This is a route-specific gap, not a claim that the

719   The thin client can retrieve incoming/outgoing adjacency records and execute bounded walks over

722   necessarily assert a logical implication. [artifact S34]
723   At the query boundary, lexical and vector hits can nominate seeds, graph navigation can expand their
724   context, and exact artifact identities can retrieve declarations. This is a proposed composition of the
725   available interfaces, not a measured fusion algorithm. A premise-admission stage must then inspect
726   source lineage, entity and temporal alignment, domain applicability, declared authority, and the
727   relevant interpretation. Keep “retrieved”, “admitted premise”, “assumed”, and “proved” as separate

744   contract than unconstrained generation of new assumptions or arbitrary executable tasks.

745   O.2   The supported-fragment Hammer workflow

746   The inspected Hammer guide describes a native goal, premise selection, translation to TPTP or SMT-
747   LIB, a bounded external portfolio, candidate normalization, reconstruction, kernel checking, and
748   receipt persistence. Each target ITP has a frontend and matching reconstructor. The first-order-safe
749   fragment admits explicit term/connective forms and finite sort signatures under the translation contract;
750   unsupported dependent types, higher-order predicate quantification, unresolved polymorphism, and
751   opaque unrecognized terms must return unsupported rather than be erased. [artifact S37]
752   All external solvers require operator opt-in. A missing native toolchain remains unavailable; a timeout
753   remains a timeout. In this ITP Hammer contract, a solver’s answer is an untrusted candidate until the
754   originating native checker accepts reconstruction. This statement is specific to that contract; another

755   SMT-facing API may report a solver-local result without representing it as a native ITP theorem.

757   A theorem applies to the formalized premises and goal. It does not independently establish that the

761   O.3    What the optional premise selector actually learns

762   The deterministic baseline uses symbol, type, import, and graph-overlap features. The optional
763   selector artifact applies a bounded linear score followed by a sigmoid, with additional count features
764   and a pinned model digest. The supplied default weights are hand-authored. Its documentation
765   explicitly does not claim a trained neural or tree-based checkpoint. A genuinely learned artifact can
766   fit the same interface, but the training dataset, loss, split, parameter provenance, and evaluation would
767   then require separate evidence. [artifact S36]
768   The documented held-out benchmark uses shared-import proximity as a relevance proxy because
769   the fixture corpus lacks explicit theorem-to-theorem dependency labels. Recall or reciprocal rank
770   against that proxy is not ground-truth proof-premise recall. A digest binds the loaded artifact; it
771   neither demonstrates training nor proves that the source of the digest was independently trusted.

772   O.4    Leanstral does not train itself by generating a draft

773   The security proof-assistant lane treats Leanstral output as advisory and checks the proposed Lean
774   code against a designated proof consumer. Its policy rejects proof bypasses such as sorry, admit,
775   or unapproved axioms. Configuration readiness, an actual model call, a generated candidate, and a
776   checker-accepted proof are different events. The source audit does not demonstrate fine-tuning of
777   Leanstral’s base weights. [artifact S39]
778   The legal rollout gives the return path to learning an explicit artifact boundary. Candidate JSON or
779   free-form drafts are audit inputs only. Structured guidance that passes the required syntax, provenance,
780   anti-copy, proof, and configured reconstruction checks may enter the trusted feature bus and bounded
781   autoencoder training. A stable source-free export remains inert until its independent promotion gate
782   accepts it. A deterministic compiler/decompiler patch is a separately versioned executable change
783   with focused tests and frozen-canary evidence. [artifact S40]
784   The documented rollout is an ordered smoke, hyperparameter-selection, fixed-canary, and production

789   P     Putting the representations and update loops together
790   This explanatory procedure composes inspected interfaces while explicitly separating design from
791   execution. It is not a claim that one repository function implements the entire procedure with every
792   optional component enabled.

793   Construct and ground. Begin with a versioned source and provenance. Retrieve candidate frames
794   or context through the configured lexical, numerical, or graph route. Compile an interpretation using
795   the chosen domain adapter and record ambiguity or unsupported semantics. Preserve the canonical
796   declaration independently of any learned feature vector. Numerical targets identify their producer;
797   compiler-derived targets identify their teacher. [artifact S01,S07,S22,S34,S35]

798   Project and challenge. Produce named logical views using shared symbols and explicit projection
799   contracts. A frame view records roles, a deontic view records normative force, a temporal view
800   records clock relations, and an event view records transitions. Source-withheld decompilation exposes
801   missing reconstructable information. Minimal pairs and independent annotations challenge omissions
802   that a compiler/decompiler cycle could share. [artifact S04,S07,S09,S31]

803   Plan, propose, and check. A query generates obligations under the relevant interpretation. The
804   Tactician selects bounded routes from admitted candidates. A Hammer can search a supported
805   translation and attempt native reconstruction; Leanstral may instead suggest or repair a candidate in an
806   approved lane. Record failure, counterexample, timeout, unsupported translation, and unavailability
807   distinctly. Only the actual checker and its pinned inputs establish the scope of a checked result.
808   [artifact S37–S39]

809   Improve the right component. An embedding residual or family/view classification defect can
810   motivate a shared-parameter update. A repeated loss of exception scope can motivate a com-
811   piler/decompiler code change. A poorly selected proof route can motivate an isolated proof-head
812   update, using only admitted eligible feedback. These diagnoses are examples of how to use the avail-
813   able signals, not measured examples of autonomous learning. Teacher fidelity, primary representation
814   quality, proof-head calibration, and executable repair correctness remain different objectives. [artifact

821   P.1   Legal, Intent, and Security IR composition

822   For the main paper’s protected-write example, a Legal declaration can express an applicable constraint,
823   an Intent declaration can describe a plan to request approval and write, and a Security declaration
824   can model whether the implementation enforces the check. An event/trace view can then describe
825   observed requests and writes. A shared name is not sufficient to join them: actor, resource, tenant,
826   operation, time, and policy-version mappings must be admitted. [artifact S01,S04,S09]
827   The plan’s postcondition is not evidence that it occurred. A permission in an intent or policy view is
828   not a record of execution. A proof that a model enforces a guard is not a legal-applicability decision.
829   The useful common space is therefore a collection of linked typed declarations and evidence records,
830   with explicit bridge obligations, rather than one blended verdict or universal latent representation.
831   A learned view head can recommend examining temporal structure, but the temporal bridge must
832   still specify its clock and scope. A graph can locate a candidate exemption provision, but the source
833   interpretation must establish applicability. A counterexample can reveal a model defect, but a changed
834   compiler must not remove the difficult premise merely to make the theorem provable. These are the
835   practical reasons for keeping retrieval, semantic interpretation, statistical guidance, and checking
836   separate while permitting structured feedback among them.
