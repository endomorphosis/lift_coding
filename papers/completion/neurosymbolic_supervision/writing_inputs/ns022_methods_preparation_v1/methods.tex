\section{Evidence and authority in the supervision loop}
\label{sec:evidence-boundaries}

A repair proposal, a successful check, permission to act, and acceptance of a
repository transition answer different questions. Our supervision architecture
represents them separately. Its semantic state contains source-linked facts,
dependencies, contracts, and unresolved obligations. Operational state records
goals, tasks, attempts, leases, and decisions. Artifact storage retains candidate
bytes, evidence, and accepted manifests. Persistence makes an observation
available for inspection; it does not establish the truth of that observation
or its applicability to a different program.

An evidence item binds a subject to a declared statement, checker, environment,
and provenance. A passing test concerns the finite checks actually executed.
A formal derivation concerns its encoded relation and premises. A permission
grant concerns an actor, action, scope, and current authority. These evidence
classes cannot substitute for one another. In particular, a signature binds a
record to a key under the signature scheme's assumptions; it does not establish
that a hidden test is adequate, that an observation is honest, or that an
arbitrary Python program satisfies an unstated specification.

The intended loop compiles obligations from current source, chooses an
admissible analysis or proposal route, checks an isolated candidate, and admits
only evidence applicable to that candidate. Unsupported translations and unknown
dependencies remain unresolved. Translation validation motivates checking a
particular conversion against its stated relation\citep{baseline04}; a
source-linked record alone is not such a validation. Likewise, abstract facts
depend on their approximation relation\citep{baseline02}. The architecture
does not infer full source-language soundness merely because a representation
is typed or content addressed.

The final experiment exercises a bounded part of this architecture: native
semantic context preparation, a model proposal, candidate custody and review,
and a separate cold-scoring path. It does not exercise the whole intended loop
as an end-to-end empirical claim. Earlier constructed integration witnesses and
component checks retain their own tested scopes. Their results cannot supply
missing final measurements of symbolic routing, policy learning, publication,
or test reuse.

\subsection{Conditional acceptance and reuse}

Proof-carrying code motivates admitting evidence against an explicit
policy\citep{baseline01}. Here, the acceptance argument is conditional: if
every successful publication checks the current parent, authorized scope,
manifest completeness, admitted evidence, and expected-parent compare-and-swap,
then every published successor satisfies those checked gates. Given a valid
initial root and exclusive use of that publication operation, induction
preserves the declared manifest policy along the accepted lineage. The
argument does not establish enforcement by unexamined write paths or atomicity
of external effects.

The reuse obligation is similarly explicit. Let the observation of test $t$
under profile $\theta$ satisfy
\[
  \operatorname{Obs}_{\theta}(t,s)=f_{\theta}(\pi_D(s))
\]
for a complete dependency projection $\pi_D$ and deterministic function
$f_{\theta}$ on every admitted state. Equality of projections then implies
equality of observations. Completeness and determinism are the difficult
premises: an incomplete dependency list, an opaque fixture, an uncontrolled
effect, or a teardown failure cannot be repaired by hashing the available
metadata. Build-system work distinguishes scheduling from deciding what must
be rebuilt\citep{baseline07}; the present reuse obligation additionally names
the statement, runtime, lifecycle, and trust conditions under which a previous
observation may transfer.

These are conditional, unmachine-checked arguments. They are not a universal
Python theorem, a newly generated native proof, or a measured reuse benefit.
The retained native-proof profile lacks the approved backend, key provenance,
and exact statement binding required for proof admission; native proving and
verification performance remain unavailable. The final comparison always uses
cold scoring and supplies no empirical test-reuse comparison.

\section{Frozen final comparison}
\label{sec:fixed-final-method}

\subsection{Population and treatment}

The retained final design contains eight recruited repository families, two
arms, and two nested repetitions per arm, for 32 fixed cells and 16 cells per
arm. The families are Tomlkit, installer, Tornado, more-itertools,
charset-normalizer, iniconfig, wheel, and Jinja. This is a convenience-selected
sample, not a representative sample of Python repositories. Repetition
identifiers 104729 and 130363 identify design positions; they are not random
seeds served to the model API.

Before final outcomes, the study retained the A/B cold comparison as an
order-preserving subset of its original 192-cell design. The other 160 factor
cells and eight unrecruited target families remain explicit missing scope.
Comparisons involving arms C/D, warm caches, test reuse, and publication
efficacy were withdrawn. The original sixteen-family inferential objectives
and D/A promotion comparison were also withdrawn; the retained A/B comparison
does not replace them with a new confirmatory claim.

Arm A supplies the selected public raw context. Arm B retains the matched raw
selection and adds actual native source-linked semantic context. Thus, the
treatment compares the combined context mechanism. It does not isolate every
semantic subcomponent or establish that a shorter prompt improves repair.
Sixteen exact request commitments bind the family/arm inputs before final
generation; the two repetitions use the corresponding fixed request. Public
source, request construction, native preparation, and their provenance remain
distinct from confidential acceptance tests.

The four-family developmental pilot is separate from the final sample. Its
24 fixed cells contain ten useful cold completions, five per arm, alongside
all failed and unscored outcomes. Earlier protocol failures and the separate
diagnostic remain retained as earlier work. Neither their outcomes nor their
costs are silently substituted for final observations.

\subsection{Proposal and cold-scoring procedure}

Each final cell permits one proposal request to Grok 4.6, with a requested
maximum output of 4,096 tokens. There is no scientific fallback model, retry,
served random-seed parameter, or reasoning-effort parameter. A failure consumes
its original cell rather than triggering a replacement. The frozen order is
executed sequentially.

Proposal execution is bounded by one CPU, 2 GiB of memory, and 32 processes.
The nominal proposal gateway and wrapper limits are 600 seconds, with a
595-second child limit and 590-second socket timeout. Actual measured intervals
determine compliance; a nominal timeout setting alone does not establish that
the boundary was met. A missing or nonfinite measurement cannot be treated as
zero or as a passing budget check.

The operator preserves exact candidate bytes and an associated AI review before
eligible scoring. This review is a procedural candidate check, not independent
human semantic annotation. Scoring uses a separate cold container with one CPU,
2 GiB of memory, no network, and 128 processes, under a 120-second host and
115-second child allowance. The scorer process limit was fixed prospectively
for both arms and all eight families because unchanged public concurrency tests
require up to 100 threads. The earlier 32-process qualification failure remains
part of the preparation record.

Useful completion requires an actually admitted candidate, nonempty public and
hidden checks all passing, and compliance with the measured resource policy.
Failed, refused, expired, unknown, unscored, or incomplete cells receive no
useful-completion credit and stay in the fixed denominator. Their distinct
failure labels and raw observations are preserved. An import or collection
failure with no hidden assertions executed is incomplete validation; it is
not evidence that hidden behavioral assertions ran and rejected the candidate.
Separate host scoring and sealed records do not establish comprehensive
resistance to adversarial scorer manipulation.

\subsection{Runtime interruption and provenance}

After the original lease ended during the seventh cell, the existing request,
failure, and cleanup evidence were retained. A separately admitted managed
lease and continuation runtime governed cells 8--32. The continuation preserved
the model, fixed requests, ordering, scorer, scientific limits, and analysis;
it did not replay the consumed prefix. Original and continued runtime source
versions remain separately identified. The failed lease record is retained,
including the unknown identity of the process that sent its termination signal.

The continuation adds runtime checks around effect admission. Their operator
phase costs are retained separately from the defined signed timing intervals.
Results must therefore retain both runtime epochs and the exact meanings of
their clocks. The prospective design record remains a pre-outcome artifact;
later execution is established by the separately retained actual records.

\section{Outcome and cost analysis}
\label{sec:frozen-analysis-method}

For family $f$, arm $a$, and repetition $r$, let $Y_{far}$ be the admitted
useful-completion indicator. The family/arm outcome averages its two fixed
repetitions, $\bar Y_{fa}=(Y_{fa1}+Y_{fa2})/2$. The retained contrast is
\[
  \widehat{\Delta}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{f=1}^{8}
       (\bar Y_{fB}-\bar Y_{fA}).
\]
The independent analysis unit is the family; 32 nested cells are not 32
independent repositories. A descriptive robust-family outcome additionally
requires both repetitions to succeed. It is not a promotion decision.

The frozen descriptive bootstrap draws eight whole family blocks with
replacement, sharing each draw across both arms and repetitions. It uses
20,000 resamples, Python 3.12's \texttt{random.Random} generator with seed
20260911, and exactly eight \texttt{randrange(8)} draws per resample. The
95\% percentile interval uses linear interpolation at index $(B-1)p$ for
$p=0.025$ and $0.975$, where $B=20{,}000$. With only eight convenience-selected
families, these are conditional descriptive intervals, not population-level
coverage or evidence for a superiority or noninferiority decision. A degenerate
interval does not establish universal equality or safety.

All failures remain in the outcome denominator. Resource summaries retain their
own available-observation counts and missingness instead of silently changing
the outcome cohort. Provider child, HTTP wrapper, gateway, scorer, and whole
operator elapsed times name different intervals. Nested intervals are not
added as disjoint costs. Native preparation shared between repetitions is
counted once; baseline qualification, operator review, queue waits, cleanup,
earlier failed attempts, and artifact work retain their own accounting scopes.

Provider token usage and cost ticks are telemetry rather than settled charges.
Unavailable remote charges remain unknown. The study has no measured human-time
endpoint, independent human semantic-fidelity annotations, or expert agreement
statistic. No result from the final A/B comparison establishes warm-cache,
publication, or proof-reuse efficiency.

\section{Reproducibility scope}
\label{sec:reproduction-scope}

The release must distinguish numerical reproduction from original-signature
verification and private-oracle rescoring. Original signed records and exact
executed source versions remain retained. Anonymous derivatives of identifying
transport metadata name both their original and derived hashes and explicitly
omit any claim that an original signature authenticates changed bytes. A
portable numerical reducer may use these manifest-bound derivative values;
agreement with the accepted analysis does not recreate a private signature or
a confidential hidden test.

Source files that require an identifying literal to be changed are labeled
public reference derivatives rather than the executed bytes. Upstream licenses
and notices remain intact. Candidate edits, public preimages, fixed revisions,
and available public-baseline instructions must retain their actual scientific
identities. Confidential oracles, signing keys, and credentials are excluded.
Stable provenance hashes permit correlation with privately retained originals,
so a lexical anonymity scan is a bounded check rather than a guarantee of
de-identification.
