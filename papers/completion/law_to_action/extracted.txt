                                                         Abstract

 1             We describe a corpus-grounded neuro-symbolic implementation for constraining
 2             Model Context Protocol (MCP) agents before protected tool execution. The method
 3             starts from three different sources: multi-jurisdictional legal documents, CVE-
 4             linked vulnerability and repair records, and SkillCenter Markdown procedures.
 5             Source-preserving adapters produce Legal IR, Security IR, and Intent IR; these
 6             share content identity and provenance but retain distinct meanings and authority.
 7             Family-specific compilers expose first-order, frame, temporal-deontic, cognitive-
 8             event, and solver-oriented views. An authorization service selects applicable
 9             constraints, constructs per-action proof obligations, and returns a context-bound de-
10             cision. A separate enforcement adapter validates the live invocation and consumes
11             a permitted use before delegation. DuckDB and a typed Quack owner maintain
12             operational state; IPFS identifies and distributes immutable evidence; libp2p car-
13             ries peer traffic; and UCAN limits delegated capabilities. We document these
14             implemented roles, the source-to-IR transformations, the separation of learned
15             proposals from checked decisions, and the tests needed to establish guarded-path
16             safety. Corpus release statistics and inspected regression assertions are reported
17             separately from end-to-end evaluation, which remains to be completed.


18   1    Introduction

19   An autonomous agent can follow a useful procedure and still violate a legal restriction, reproduce
20   a known vulnerability, or act outside its delegated authority. A safeguard placed only in the model
21   prompt cannot distinguish these cases reliably. The implementation described here places source-
22   grounded constraints and a separate authorization boundary between proposed actions and protected
23   MCP handlers. MCP supplies tool discovery and invocation; application implementations remain
24   responsible for enforcing security requirements [1, 2].
25   Our method begins before a tool call. A legal collection supplies normative constraints, a vulnerability-
26   and-fix collection supplies security evidence, and a skill collection supplies descriptions of intended
27   procedures. These become three intermediate representations (IRs), not one undifferentiated retrieval
28   index. Formalization produces typed logic views and proof obligations. The runtime joins the results
29   against one exact invocation, then checks authorization again before the effect. This design combines
30   semantic compilation, proof search, capability security, and stateful execution rather than asking a
31   language model to certify its own behavior.
32   We document four implemented contributions: (1) source-preserving adapters and retrieval releases
33   for the three IR families; (2) multi-logic constraint compilation and proof-oriented authorization;
34   (3) DuckDB-backed supervision and exact-context enforcement; and (4) content-addressed, peer-
35   accessible evidence and delegated MCP execution. Legal interpretation and learned extraction remain

38   2     Corpus sources and their compilation into IRs

39   2.1   Legal documents to Legal IR

40   The legal collection is an input to the reasoning system, not an application mentioned after the
41   architecture. Its source families include the United States Code, state statutes, state administrative
42   rules, Federal Register documents, state and local court rules, municipal and county codes, caselaw,
43   and national legislation outside the United States. The collection is expanding internationally;
44   complete worldwide coverage is a collection objective, not an assumption of the method. The
45   canonical registry binds each supported corpus to a country/branch, published dataset identifier, local
46   root, JSON-LD layout, Parquet layout, and source-specific identity fields.
47   The ingestion path preserves original documents separately from normalized provisions. Official
48   sources and archive-derived captures retain their origin, retrieval revision, extraction route, and
49   coverage limitations. For example, an international national-legislation snapshot can be recovered
50   from Common Crawl WARC and Wayback captures of an official publisher. That is a provenance-
51   bearing archive route, not a claim that an archive capture is a newly issued official text. Federal
52   Register proposals, notices, and final rules must likewise remain different source kinds. Legal
53   applicability depends on jurisdiction, authority, effective interval, definitions, exceptions, and the
54   facts of the request, not merely textual similarity.
55   The published retrieval layers add CID-keyed records, sparse indexes, vectors, and graph links. They
56   are views of a documentary substrate. A statutory citation edge is not a proved implication, an
57   embedding neighbor is not controlling authority, and a release named “IR” may contain retrieval
58   records rather than completed formal proofs. The corpus manifest must therefore count source
59   records, derived views, rejected records, and checked formalizations separately. Appendix A gives
60   source families, representative release pins, and the exact questions a coverage manifest must answer.
61   The legal compiler has two distinguishable paths. The rich deontic bridge preserves actor, action,
62   modality, conditions, exceptions, temporal expressions, definitions, references, available source spans,
63   and diagnostics. The stricter canonical compiler maps selected norms into a bounded vocabulary
64   with seven fields: modality, actor, action, object, conditions, exceptions, and temporal qualifiers.
65   Unrepresented material cannot disappear silently into a successful result. Source maps, declared
66   losses, producer/configuration identity, and review state travel with the derived object. Compilation
67   is thus reproducible and inspectable, while source-to-rule fidelity remains a separate validation
68   requirement. Executable-law work such as Catala supplies a relevant precedent, but our contribution
69   also includes runtime agent and capability boundaries [3].


70   2.2   Vulnerability and repair evidence to Security IR

71   The security source is not simply a list of CVE identifiers. CVEfixes links vulnerability records
72   to source repositories and fixes, supporting inspection of vulnerable and changed code [4]. Our
73   inspected release uses an exact pinned hitoshura25/cvefixes snapshot, preserves the original
74   Parquet bytes, and provides a source-CID-to-original-row index. The release reports 12,987 input
75   rows, 12,714 derived records, and 273 tombstones; these are release counts, not distinct-vulnerability
76   counts or verified-prohibition counts. Original evidence and derived outputs remain independently
77   addressable.
78   The conversion is explicit: a bounded source record and a policy candidate, together with their
79   review binding, enter adapt_cvefixes_candidate. The adapter constructs a canonical Security
80   IR declaration, including an exact scoped deny-policy candidate where representable. Its contract
81   rejects wildcard/generalized scope and attempts to insert an authority grant into the candidate.
82   Evaluation results remain separate from declarations. The formalization adapter then lowers the
83   declaration into policy, threat, transition, and claim views. The emitted prohibition binds action,
84   preconditions, effects, mitigations, language, scope, and CVE/CWE identifiers. A CVE or CWE
85   label alone is not the policy.

86    Vulnerable and fixed examples have different methodological roles. Vulnerable code is a positive
87    control for a candidate security restriction; fixed code is a negative control for that particular restriction.
88    A fix does not certify that all other vulnerabilities are absent. The formalization emits a candidate
89    prohibition and proof obligations, not a theorem or an execution denial merely because a record was
90    retrieved.
91    The runtime connection is concrete. cve_security_gate maps both Intent IR actions and generated-
92    code fact groups into exact SecurityAuthorizationRequest objects under the same pinned Security IR
93    root. It correlates the two streams. A harmless declared intent cannot mask an undeclared, broader,
94    contradictory, or rejected effect in generated code. Incomplete mappings remain unknown. The
95    hard-constraint compiler consumes these checks before quality or cost ranking; no favorable score
96    offsets a failed security constraint.

97    2.3   SkillCenter Markdown to Intent IR

98    SkillCenter supplies procedural source material: skill_md, library_md, YAML metadata, titles,
99    domains, profiles, original URLs, source identifiers, and licensing information. The bounded reader
100   opens upstream SQLite bundles read-only and immutable, disables extension loading, checks expected
101   tables and columns, and applies record-size limits. SQLite here is an upstream corpus container,
102   not the agent supervisor’s state database. Markdown bodies are parsed as untrusted data; commands
103   appearing in a skill are never executed by ingestion.
104   The pinned SkillCenter build reports 216,972 physical records in 24 bundles. The implementation
105   explicitly reconciles physical counts against inconsistent upstream metadata. It computes two useful
106   identities: an entry_cid over intrinsic record content and an exact body CID over UTF-8 skill_md.
107   Container filename and dataset-revision provenance are retained separately so that repackaging
108   unchanged intrinsic content does not change its entry identity. The derived release uses these
109   identities across Parquet records, BM25 postings, graph nodes, vectors, and source lookup.
110   Intent IR describes what a procedure tries to accomplish. Its schema distinguishes goals, preconditions,
111   postconditions, invariants, guards, effects, assumptions, failures, and verification steps. Actions refer
112   to tools, inputs, outputs, and required evidence. Control edges distinguish next, success, failure,
113   conditional, retry, parallel, and join relationships. Source spans and grounded-versus-inferred status
114   accompany semantic nodes. A procedure’s word “permitted” is a source assertion, not a capability
115   from the system owner.
116   The full retrieval corpus must not be confused with an exhaustively formalized skill library. The
117   source adapter, schema, index builders, and formalizer/normalizer ports are inspectable; every claimed
118   normalized document still needs its source and validation record. Learned normalization and a trained
119   semantic encoder operate behind those ports. Corpus size alone says neither that every procedure has
120   been normalized nor that every normalized procedure is safe to execute.

121   2.4   Common lineage without collapsing the domains

122   The three families share ir_core canonicalization, identity, provenance, schema, claim, obligation,
123   and result contracts. They do not share an ontology or grant authority to one another. The kernel sep-
124   arates declarations, runs, results, and receipts. Running a prover therefore creates a new result/receipt
125   rather than mutating the underlying legal norm or skill declaration.

131   3     Logic families and executable proof work
132   3.1   Why the implementation has several logics

133   An IR is a domain representation; a logic family determines how its statements are interpreted. A
134   prover is a backend that checks a supported encoding. Keeping these concepts separate is essential. A
135   temporal obligation cannot become an ordinary Boolean predicate without recording what temporal
136   meaning was lost. The compiler therefore produces native views, shared symbol bindings, source
137   maps, and explicit cross-view links rather than concatenating formulas from different logics.

138   Temporal Deontic First-Order Logic (TDFOL) explicitly combines first-order predicates and quanti-
139   fiers with obligation/permission/prohibition and temporal operators such as always, eventually, next,
140   until, and since. Deontic Cognitive Event Calculus (DCEC) adds cognitive operators including belief,
141   knowledge, intention, desire, and goal alongside event and normative structure. These are useful
142   distinctions for agents: a model’s belief that a request is authorized is not an authenticated fact that it
143   is authorized. Encoding a knowledge operator does not validate a perception result or make generated
144   text true.
145   F-logic contributes object-centered semantic relations, not digital signatures. Event calculus is the
146   reasoning vocabulary for events and changing fluents; the append-only execution Event DAG is its

148   occur in the broader training/representation contracts. We do not infer live proof support from those
149   tags. Appendix B records the role, supported scope, and appropriate evidence boundary for each
150   family.

151   3.2   Translation, solving, reconstruction, and reuse

152   Each backend request binds its family, premises, assumptions, target obligation, supported fragment,
153   and resource limits. Z3 and cvc5 supply SMT results, models, or cores; Vampire and E serve first-
154   order automated proving paths; interactive theorem-prover adapters support proof reconstruction and
155   kernel checking. Hammers select premises, translate to TPTP or SMT-LIB, run candidate-producing
156   portfolios, and reconstruct a native proof where supported. Solver success and kernel acceptance
157   remain different evidence kinds.
158   The verification API also exposes abstract interpretation, assume-guarantee discharge, incremental
159   SMT, differential checking, interpolation, and counterexample handling. These are selected methods,
160   not a checklist run on every invocation. For example, the inspected interpolation path is bounded to
161   QF_LIA with cvc5 production and independent checks of both interpolation conditions and shared
162   vocabulary. An unavailable interpolant can produce a separately labeled validated core; it cannot be
163   reported as a successful interpolant.
164   Proof-grounded IR learning uses source/IR, translation, reconstruction, proof, tactic, and coun-
165   terexample traces. Its contracts distinguish structural equality, logical equivalence, entailment,
166   equisatisfiability, and heuristic similarity. A learned encoder can nominate a representation or
167   premise set; the declared checker supplies acceptance evidence. Translation and reconstruction
168   receipts retain the weakest relevant boundary. A nearest-neighbor score, a successful paraphrase, or a
169   corpus-membership proof cannot upgrade a candidate to a theorem.


170   4     Joining intent, law, and security before execution

171   4.1   Concrete invocation and hard-constraint admission

172   An InvocationIntentEnvelope commits to source identity, tenant, actor, audience, delegation,
173   tool/version, arguments, proposed effects, environment, nonce, and relevant roots. Trusted adapters
174   bind the effect description to the actual operation. Otherwise an agent could authorize a harmless
175   description and execute a different effect.
176   Legal and security applicability are evaluated independently. Legal queries bind jurisdiction, subject,
177   authority, time, actor, resource, definitions, cross-references, exceptions, and source integrity. Security
178   queries bind principal, capability, trust zone, asset, channel, action, effect, failure/rollback, and
179   environment evidence. Hard filters precede ranking. Missing or contradictory required evidence is
180   retained as a blocking condition rather than removed to make a request pass.
181   The supervisor’s IRConstraintCompiler joins these results against the exact candidate ac-
182   tion/effect graph. It rejects undeclared effects, unmatched domain bindings, incomplete legal applica-
183   bility, applicable prohibitions, unresolved obligations, denied/unknown/conflicting security decisions,
184   stale roots, unsatisfied dependencies, missing proofs, and failed validation. Intent conformance and
185   legal permission constrain the candidate but do not grant security authorization. The output includes
186   rejection witnesses and the affected action set for local replanning rather than a scalar score that a
187   model can optimize around.

188   4.2   Authorization proof jobs and decision receipts

189   AuthorizationQueryComposer generates per-action jobs for applicability, positive grant, non-
190   conflict, security invariant, pre-obligation, coverage, and context binding. Coverage is relative
191   to the declared modeled scope, not proof that all relevant law has been collected. Additional
192   jobs can address consistency, translation, reconstruction, and obligations during or after execu-
193   tion. AuthorizationPortfolio records attempts and selects compatible results deterministically;
194   contradictory authoritative results require review.


                                                          5
195   IntentAuthorizationService composes normalization, lowering, evidence selection, jobs, port-
196   folio execution, decision, receipt, and optional internal capability derivation. It does not execute the re-
197   quested tool. Unknown, unsupported, contradictory, SAT-only, simulated, and merely policy/evidence-
198   ready outcomes cannot silently satisfy the proof-oriented allow policy. An AttestedProofVerifier
199   checks selected evidence against exact statements, assumptions, source/build/toolchain identities,
200   obligations, scope, and corpus/policy/revocation roots. Signed assertions and zero-knowledge artifacts
201   retain the statement and verifier they actually attest.


202   4.3   Pre-invocation enforcement

203   A receipt becomes operational only through a protected effect boundary.
204   SupervisorPreInvocationEnforcement.authorize_and_delegate in enforce mode
205   rechecks the live invocation, outcome, effects, expiry, roots, and capability, then atomically consumes
206   the permitted use before invoking the delegate. Off, audit, and shadow modes do not provide the
207   same prevention semantics. The supplied clock, roots, receipt issuer, and consumption store must be
208   trusted and appropriate to the deployment.
209   The separate short-lived ExecutionPermit path also binds the admitted graph, roots, mandatory
210   dependency closure, validation plan, caller, policy, lease, fence, expiry, and use bound. Plan admission
211   is not execution permission, and permission is not completion evidence. These interfaces must be
212   traced at each entry point: a lightweight Profile D dispatcher, a UCAN-gated MCP server, and a
213   full proof-oriented wrapper have related but nonidentical checks. Our security claim concerns a
214   configured, mediated path, not every historical compatibility route.



215   5     DuckDB and event-driven operational state

216   The supervisor’s mutable control state is DuckDB, specifically control.duckdb, behind the typed
217   Quack state-owner path. This is distinct from immutable IPFS artifacts and from read-only source-
218   corpus containers. DuckDB/Quack provides the database access substrate [5]; the implementation
219   adds ownership, command validation, generation/fence checks, and bounded retries.
220   QuackStateClient exposes closed, named parameter-bound commands rather than model-supplied
221   SQL. The inspected client supports an embedded exclusive-lock mode for hermetic/single-process
222   tooling and a Quack-mode typed Unix-socket owner gateway. It verifies store/server identity, schema
223   fingerprint, generation, and extension fingerprint. Generic client ATTACH and arbitrary SQL are not
224   the model-facing interface. The operational boundary is the project’s typed service, not unrestricted
225   access to every feature supported by the underlying Quack extension.
226   StateTransaction binds expected store generation, fence epoch, row revision, and idempotency
227   identity in short transactions. Same-row optimistic conflicts can receive bounded jittered retries;
228   stale generation, mismatched fence, and changed-payload idempotency conflicts are non-retryable
229   authority failures. A lost reply resolves against the committed command identity rather than causing
230   the LLM to repeat an effect. Database transitions are authoritative after commit. Event/outbox/cursor
231   integration and recovery must be qualified for the selected owner deployment; they are not replaced
232   by Markdown task-board edits.
233   Provider processes do not receive the owner’s token or an open SQL capability. The threat model
234   prohibits extension installation, file-reader escapes, cross-root operations, and Python UDF surfaces
235   through the typed client. Offline maintenance requires exclusive ownership. This is a single-owner
236   failure domain, not multi-primary consensus. DuckLake projections or exported reports can support
237   analysis but do not acquire task-mutation authority.
238   Source, proof, policy, capability, and completion events trigger rescanning and targeted invalidation.
239   Relevant changes reopen affected obligations; unchanged semantic dependencies remain candidates
240   for reuse. The supervisor’s generation and fencing mechanisms prevent an old worker from publishing
241   its result over newer state. Durable capability consumption and uncertain remote-effect recovery
242   must be tested in the actual executor: a DuckDB commit alone does not make an arbitrary remote
243   API exactly once.

244   6     IPFS, libp2p, UCAN, and MCP++ in the execution path
245   6.1   IPFS: identity and distribution of evidence

246   The InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) uses content addressing to name bytes by content rather than
247   by a mutable location [6]. Kit stores source bodies, IR declarations, graphs, ContextPacks, proofs,
248   source/alias maps, decisions, and receipts as immutable artifacts. A recipient rehashes the retrieved
249   bytes under the declared profile. Canonical declarations bind domain and schema so equal-looking
250   payloads from different families cannot substitute for one another.
251   The IR kernel uses a declared canonical-JSON identity envelope and CIDv1/raw/SHA-256 profile;
252   other contracts, including Profile D artifacts, use DAG-JSON. The codec and preimage are part
253   of the contract. A plain SHA-256 digest is not relabeled a CID. A graph root commits to its
254   referenced closure, while mutable current roots advance through a controlled publication path. This
255   enables reproducible evidence reuse across machines. Availability still requires retention/pinning and
256   functioning peers; local CID computation proves neither publication nor public availability. Private
257   evidence needs explicit access and confidentiality controls.

258   6.2   libp2p: peer communication, not permission

259   libp2p supplies peer identities, transport selection, secure channels, protocol negotiation, and multi-
260   plexed streams [7]. A Peer ID identifies a key-bound peer; it is not an application user or a tool grant.
261   The MCP++ P2P carriage profile uses /mcp+p2p/1.0.0 with framed MCP messages. Stdio, HTTP,
262   and P2P adapters should converge on the same validated dispatch semantics rather than implement
263   different authorization policies.
264   The relevant architectural benefit is that another agent can exchange an objective, request an IR
265   artifact by CID, obtain proof evidence, or call a permitted tool without sharing the supervisor database.
266   Discovery or a successfully authenticated peer connection does not authorize those operations. Tests
267   of a local stream framing path also must not be reported as a wide-area network or adversarial
268   transport qualification.

269   6.3   UCAN: attenuated delegated authority

270   The User Controlled Authorization Network (UCAN) represents signed delegated capabilities [8]. In
271   the inspected strict verifier, Ed25519 signatures, issuer/audience continuity, parent proofs, explicit
272   resource/ability fields, validity windows, and supported request bounds are checked. A child capability
273   must remain within the parent’s authority. A complete path segment matters: tenant-a/* must not
274   accidentally include tenant-ab. The durable revocation/replay ledger supplies state that a signature
275   alone cannot provide.
276   This is separate from a formal proof. UCAN answers whether an authenticated principal may exercise
277   a delegated capability. Legal and security reasoning answer whether this particular operation satisfies
278   the applicable modeled constraints. Both must be satisfied where required. A parsed unsigned
279   delegation stub, an agent-authored permission sentence, or a valid signature on irrelevant bytes cannot
280   replace the strict verifier. The proof-derived AuthorizationCapability and a signed UCAN token are
281   also distinct formats; deployments must connect them through an explicit trusted binding rather than
282   equating their names.

283   6.4   MCP++: where these mechanisms meet

284   MCP++ adds optional profiles around MCP rather than replacing JSON-RPC. Profile A identifies the
285   interface; B binds invocation and result artifacts; C specifies delegation; D carries temporal-deontic
286   policy; E carries P2P messages; F records causal provenance; and G describes tasks, claims, leases,
287   and coordination. Their portable schemas support heterogeneous agents, while the implementations
288   perform the checks.
289   Kit’s protected tools/call route invokes AuthorizationGate before dispatch. The gate checks
290   envelope/tool/resource/ability bindings, a concrete UCAN verifier and ledger, and the Datasets-
291   backed Profile D provider, then records the decision. Missing validators or required authorities
292   deny. Profile D matches actor/action/resource/time, applies prohibition precedence, and requires a


                                                         7
293   matching permission. A returned obligation needs a handling policy; the inspected strict Kit gate
294   accepts plain allow, not unresolved allow_with_obligations. This is a concrete policy gate.
295   Stronger theorem-required actions additionally need the proof-oriented enforcement path; carrying a
296   proof_cid in an output envelope does not establish that it was checked before execution.

297   7   Worked source-to-effect trace and evaluation
298   Consider an agent that retrieves a Markdown procedure for exporting records. The following is a
299   composition walkthrough, not a newly executed integration result. The source record contributes
300   a procedure and source spans, not permission. Intent IR describes the selected records, recipi-
301   ent/channel, expected effects, prerequisites, and verification. The legal slice supplies applicable
302   reviewed disclosure/retention constraints. A retrieved vulnerability/fix example contributes a scoped
303   candidate constraint for an unsafe data-flow or missing validation; applicability and review determine
304   whether it participates. Generated code is independently analyzed so that its effects can be correlated
305   with the stated intent.
306   The supervisor binds this candidate to current Legal/Security/Intent roots and composes the required
307   proof jobs. An unapproved destination, an unresolved exception, or an undeclared code effect blocks
308   admission. A permissible candidate still requires a current grant for the exact actor, tool, resource,
309   and bounds. The protected runtime checks the receipt and capability immediately before delegation;
310   a recipient change invalidates the argument commitment, while revocation or consumed-use state
311   rejects replay. DuckDB records operational progression, and immutable evidence records explain
312   which sources, rules, proofs, and grants led to the result. The model can propose a revised plan, but it
313   cannot remove a failed obligation by rewriting its explanation.
314   The evaluation should exercise these existing paths. Source tests cover bounded SkillCenter reading,
315   deterministic identities, CVE scope and authority rejection, typed compilation, proof-result authority,
316   enforce-mode non-delegation, replay/concurrent consumption, Quack command/fence checks, and
317   MCP transport parity. Inspected test assertions are not executed results. The next frozen experiment
318   must record actual results separately for fixture, local solver, cryptographic, storage, and end-to-end
319   agent paths.
320   Compare unguarded sandbox execution, retrieval plus prompt instructions, lightweight policy/UCAN
321   gating, and full proof-oriented enforcement on the same handlers. Cross-source cases should
322   include malicious skill text, a wrong-jurisdiction or stale legal version, a misleading CVE match,
323   vulnerable/fixed controls, omitted code effects, a widened capability, changed arguments, and restarted
324   state. Measure actual forbidden delegate effects together with allowed-task success, false denial,
325   abstention, source/formalization fidelity, route coverage, tokens, proof cost, and latency. No numerical
326   safety or cost advantage is asserted before these runs. Appendices provide the lineage and experiment
327   fields.

328   8   Scope and conclusion
329   The implemented contribution is a source-grounded constraint pipeline and a controllable action
330   boundary. It does not require a complete global corpus to enforce a reviewed bounded policy, and a
331   large corpus does not prove completeness. Formalization may be partial; model proposals, signatures,
332   content identities, monitor observations, solver results, and kernel proofs retain distinct meanings.
333   Complete mediation, trusted current roots, authenticated issuers, and appropriate persistence remain
334   deployment requirements.
335   The system is best understood as a compiler-and-runtime stack: Legal IR supplies normative con-
336   straints, Security IR supplies behavior and threat constraints, and Intent IR supplies proposed
337   procedures. Native logics and proof tools determine what evidence is needed; DuckDB maintains
338   operational truth; IPFS preserves exact artifacts; libp2p carries peer interactions; and UCAN limits
339   delegated authority. The safety-relevant result is not a more confident explanation. It is whether a
340   non-admitted action is prevented from reaching a protected handler.

450   B.2     Proof workflow and result classes

451   The common proof workflow is: choose a supported view; create a typed obligation and premises;
452   select a backend/hammer plan; execute within a bound; preserve translations, models, cores, and
453   proof candidates; reconstruct where required; run the declared checker; then emit a result and receipt.
454   Z3/cvc5, Vampire/E, and Lean/Coq/Isabelle belong to different stages. A timeout or unavailable
455   backend is not a refutation, and satisfiability of a favorable instance does not prove universal safety.
456   Datasets distinguishes authority kinds such as theorem proof, satisfiability, runtime monitor, evidence
457   readiness, and policy approval. These are non-interchangeable. The supervisor also uses an assurance
458   classification for whether the evidence meets a requested threshold. Neither mechanism permits a
459   provider to upgrade its own output by naming it “verified.” A cryptographic attestation must bind the
460   actual checked claim, verifier and public inputs; proving corpus membership alone cannot prove the
461   member’s theorem.
462   Counterexample-guided refinement identifies missing facts or assumptions in the encoded problem
463   and requests specific additional context. Interpolation and unsat cores can narrow that request; they
464   do not discover an omitted statute automatically. Abstract interpretation overapproximates a qualified
465   program fragment, while assume-guarantee discharge checks that a producer’s established guarantee
466   entails a consumer’s assumption. Learned premise/tactic selection and proof-grounded encoders
467   improve search proposals; independent checking remains the acceptance operation.

470   C.2     A state transition through the typed owner

471   A caller supplies a named command and typed parameters, not a SQL string. The owner validates
472   store identity, schema/generation, task revision, fence, and idempotency key. A successful transaction
473   commits the permitted state change and its recorded command result. The identical command can
474   retrieve that result after a lost reply; a changed payload with the same identity is a conflict. Stale
475   generations and fences require new admission rather than blind retry. Event delivery can wake
476   dependent work, but does not substitute for the committed state.
477   The inspected QuackStateClient describes both an embedded mode and a typed owner-gateway mode.
478   Documentation that discusses generic Quack/ATTACH capability must not override the current
479   client’s deliberately narrower interface. Likewise, the architecture should not promise that a reference
480   in-memory capability consumption store is already backed by DuckDB. A deployment must supply
481   and test the actual durable adapter used for receipt/capability consumption.

482   C.3     IPFS and libp2p are complementary

483   IPFS answers which artifact bytes are requested and enables verifiable retrieval of that content.
484   Canonical IR, a source document, a decision receipt, and a graph node can each have independent
485   identities. Pin/retention and transport choices determine availability. Public availability is not the
486   default requirement for private legal or security material.
487   libp2p answers how peers connect, authenticate a peer key, negotiate an application protocol, and
488   exchange framed streams. A protocol ID selects the decoder; it does not select a legal jurisdiction
489   or an authorization profile by itself. TLS/Noise or native transport security protects a channel when
490   configured; it does not turn a peer’s proof claim into a checked proof. Deployments must record
491   actual transports and security settings. Do not count an in-process call to a P2P handler as a full
492   libp2p network experiment.

493   C.4     UCAN and proof-oriented capabilities are distinct

494   Strict UCAN checks cryptographic issuer identity, delegation continuity, explicit resource/ability
495   bounds, temporal validity, permitted attenuation, revocation, and replay policy. It does not infer
496   delegation from natural-language text. A principal may possess a valid capability yet fail a legal or
497   security obligation. Conversely, a mathematically valid proof of a property does not authorize the
498   principal to invoke an administrative tool.
499   The proof-oriented AuthorizationCapability is an additional exact-context contract. Its relationship
500   to a UCAN envelope must bind the same actor/audience, tool/version, arguments, effects, policy
501   roots, and allowed use. Hash equality checks identity; authentication must come from the trusted
502   issuer/transport mechanism. A signed token, capability use record, and proof receipt are related
503   evidence objects, not aliases for one another.
