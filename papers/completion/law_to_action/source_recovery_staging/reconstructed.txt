                                                         Abstract

 1             We describe a corpus-grounded neuro-symbolic implementation for constraining
 2             Model Context Protocol (MCP) agents before protected tool execution. The method
 3             starts from three different sources: multi-jurisdictional legal documents, CVE-
 4             linked vulnerability and repair records, and SkillCenter Markdown procedures.
 5             Source-preserving adapters produce Legal IR, Security IR, and Intent IR; these
 6             share content identity and provenance but retain distinct meanings and authority.
 7             Family-specific compilers expose first-order, frame, temporal-deontic, cognitive-
 8             event, and solver-oriented views. An authorization service selects applicable
 9             constraints, constructs per-action proof obligations, and returns a context-bound de-
10             cision. A separate enforcement adapter validates the live invocation and consumes
11             a permitted use before delegation. DuckDB and a typed Quack owner maintain
12             operational state; IPFS identifies and distributes immutable evidence; libp2p car-
13             ries peer traffic; and UCAN limits delegated capabilities. We document these
14             implemented roles, the source-to-IR transformations, the separation of learned
15             proposals from checked decisions, and the tests needed to establish guarded-path
16             safety. Corpus release statistics and inspected regression assertions are reported
17             separately from end-to-end evaluation, which remains to be completed.


18   1    Introduction

19   An autonomous agent can follow a useful procedure and still violate a legal restriction, reproduce
20   a known vulnerability, or act outside its delegated authority. A safeguard placed only in the model
21   prompt cannot distinguish these cases reliably. The implementation described here places source-
22   grounded constraints and a separate authorization boundary between proposed actions and protected
23   MCP handlers. MCP supplies tool discovery and invocation; application implementations remain
24   responsible for enforcing security requirements [1, 2].
25   Our method begins before a tool call. A legal collection supplies normative constraints, a vulnerability-
26   and-fix collection supplies security evidence, and a skill collection supplies descriptions of intended
27   procedures. These become three intermediate representations (IRs), not one undifferentiated retrieval
28   index. Formalization produces typed logic views and proof obligations. The runtime joins the results
29   against one exact invocation, then checks authorization again before the effect. This design combines
30   semantic compilation, proof search, capability security, and stateful execution rather than asking a
31   language model to certify its own behavior.
32   We document four implemented contributions: (1) source-preserving adapters and retrieval releases
33   for the three IR families; (2) multi-logic constraint compilation and proof-oriented authorization;
34   (3) DuckDB-backed supervision and exact-context enforcement; and (4) content-addressed, peer-
35   accessible evidence and delegated MCP execution. Legal interpretation and learned extraction remain

38   2     Corpus sources and their compilation into IRs
39   2.1   Legal documents to Legal IR

40   The legal collection is an input to the reasoning system, not an application mentioned after the
41   architecture. Its source families include the United States Code, state statutes, state administrative
42   rules, Federal Register documents, state and local court rules, municipal and county codes, caselaw,
43   and national legislation outside the United States. The collection is expanding internationally;
44   complete worldwide coverage is a collection objective, not an assumption of the method. The
45   canonical registry binds each supported corpus to a country/branch, published dataset identifier, local
46   root, JSON-LD layout, Parquet layout, and source-specific identity fields.
47   The ingestion path preserves original documents separately from normalized provisions. Official
48   sources and archive-derived captures retain their origin, retrieval revision, extraction route, and
49   coverage limitations. For example, an international national-legislation snapshot can be recovered
50   from Common Crawl WARC and Wayback captures of an official publisher. That is a provenance-
51   bearing archive route, not a claim that an archive capture is a newly issued official text. Federal
52   Register proposals, notices, and final rules must likewise remain different source kinds. Legal
53   applicability depends on jurisdiction, authority, effective interval, definitions, exceptions, and the
54   facts of the request, not merely textual similarity.
55   The published retrieval layers add CID-keyed records, sparse indexes, vectors, and graph links. They
56   are views of a documentary substrate. A statutory citation edge is not a proved implication, an
57   embedding neighbor is not controlling authority, and a release named “IR” may contain retrieval
58   records rather than completed formal proofs. The corpus manifest must therefore count source
59   records, derived views, rejected records, and checked formalizations separately. Appendix A gives
60   source families, representative release pins, and the exact questions a coverage manifest must answer.
61   The legal compiler has two distinguishable paths. The rich deontic bridge preserves actor, action,
62   modality, conditions, exceptions, temporal expressions, definitions, references, available source spans,
63   and diagnostics. The stricter canonical compiler maps selected norms into a bounded vocabulary
64   with seven fields: modality, actor, action, object, conditions, exceptions, and temporal qualifiers.
65   Unrepresented material cannot disappear silently into a successful result. Source maps, declared
66   losses, producer/configuration identity, and review state travel with the derived object. Compilation
67   is thus reproducible and inspectable, while source-to-rule fidelity remains a separate validation
68   requirement. Executable-law work such as Catala supplies a relevant precedent, but our contribution
69   also includes runtime agent and capability boundaries [3].

70   2.2   Vulnerability and repair evidence to Security IR

71   The security source is not simply a list of CVE identifiers. CVEfixes links vulnerability records to
72   source repositories and fixes, supporting inspection of vulnerable and changed code [4]. Our inspected
73   release uses an exact pinned hitoshura25/cvefixes snapshot, preserves the original Parquet bytes,
74   and provides a source-CID-to-original-row index. The release reports 12,987 input rows, 12,714
75   derived records, and 273 tombstones; these are release counts, not distinct-vulnerability counts or
76   verified-prohibition counts. Original evidence and derived outputs remain independently addressable.
77   The conversion is explicit: a bounded source record and a policy candidate, together with their
78   review binding, enter adapt_cvefixes_candidate. The adapter constructs a canonical Security IR
79   declaration, including an exact scoped deny-policy candidate where representable. Its contract rejects
80   wildcard/generalized scope and attempts to insert an authority grant into the candidate. Evaluation
81   results remain separate from declarations. The formalization adapter then lowers the declaration
82   into policy, threat, transition, and claim views. The emitted prohibition binds action, preconditions,
83   effects, mitigations, language, scope, and CVE/CWE identifiers. A CVE or CWE label alone is not
84   the policy.
85   Vulnerable and fixed examples have different methodological roles. Vulnerable code is a positive
86   control for a candidate security restriction; fixed code is a negative control for that particular restriction.

 88   prohibition and proof obligations, not a theorem or an execution denial merely because a record was
 89   retrieved.
 90   The runtime connection is concrete. cve_security_gate maps both Intent IR actions and generated-
 91   code fact groups into exact SecurityAuthorizationRequest objects under the same pinned Security IR
 92   root. It correlates the two streams. A harmless declared intent cannot mask an undeclared, broader,
 93   contradictory, or rejected effect in generated code. Incomplete mappings remain unknown. The
 94   hard-constraint compiler consumes these checks before quality or cost ranking; no favorable score
 95   offsets a failed security constraint.

 96   2.3   SkillCenter Markdown to Intent IR

 97   SkillCenter supplies procedural source material: skill_md, library_md, YAML metadata, titles,
 98   domains, profiles, original URLs, source identifiers, and licensing information. The bounded reader
 99   opens upstream SQLite bundles read-only and immutable, disables extension loading, checks expected
100   tables and columns, and applies record-size limits. SQLite here is an upstream corpus container, not
101   the agent supervisor’s state database. Markdown bodies are parsed as untrusted data; commands
102   appearing in a skill are never executed by ingestion.
103   The pinned SkillCenter build reports 216,972 physical records in 24 bundles. The implementation
104   explicitly reconciles physical counts against inconsistent upstream metadata. It computes two useful
105   identities: an entry_cid over intrinsic record content and an exact body CID over UTF-8 skill_
106   md. Container filename and dataset-revision provenance are retained separately so that repackaging
107   unchanged intrinsic content does not change its entry identity. The derived release uses these identities
108   across Parquet records, BM25 postings, graph nodes, vectors, and source lookup.
109   Intent IR describes what a procedure tries to accomplish. Its schema distinguishes goals, preconditions,
110   postconditions, invariants, guards, effects, assumptions, failures, and verification steps. Actions refer
111   to tools, inputs, outputs, and required evidence. Control edges distinguish next, success, failure,
112   conditional, retry, parallel, and join relationships. Source spans and grounded-versus-inferred status
113   accompany semantic nodes. A procedure’s word “permitted” is a source assertion, not a capability
114   from the system owner.
115   The full retrieval corpus must not be confused with an exhaustively formalized skill library. The
116   source adapter, schema, index builders, and formalizer/normalizer ports are inspectable; every claimed
117   normalized document still needs its source and validation record. Learned normalization and a trained
118   semantic encoder operate behind those ports. Corpus size alone says neither that every procedure has
119   been normalized nor that every normalized procedure is safe to execute.

120   2.4   Common lineage without collapsing the domains

121   The three families share ir_core canonicalization, identity, provenance, schema, claim, obligation, and
122   result contracts. They do not share an ontology or grant authority to one another. The kernel separates
123   declarations, runs, results, and receipts. Running a prover therefore creates a new result/receipt rather
124   than mutating the underlying legal norm or skill declaration.

130   3     Logic families and executable proof work

131   3.1   Why the implementation has several logics

132   An IR is a domain representation; a logic family determines how its statements are interpreted. A
133   prover is a backend that checks a supported encoding. Keeping these concepts separate is essential. A
134   temporal obligation cannot become an ordinary Boolean predicate without recording what temporal
135   meaning was lost. The compiler therefore produces native views, shared symbol bindings, source
136   maps, and explicit cross-view links rather than concatenating formulas from different logics.

137   Temporal Deontic First-Order Logic (TDFOL) explicitly combines first-order predicates and quanti-
138   fiers with obligation/permission/prohibition and temporal operators such as always, eventually, next,
139   until, and since. Deontic Cognitive Event Calculus (DCEC) adds cognitive operators including belief,
140   knowledge, intention, desire, and goal alongside event and normative structure. These are useful
141   distinctions for agents: a model’s belief that a request is authorized is not an authenticated fact that it
142   is authorized. Encoding a knowledge operator does not validate a perception result or make generated
143   text true.
144   F-logic contributes object-centered semantic relations, not digital signatures. Event calculus is the
145   reasoning vocabulary for events and changing fluents; the append-only execution Event DAG is its
146   provenance substrate, not itself an event-calculus proof. Higher-order, linear, and separation tags also
147   occur in the broader training/representation contracts. We do not infer live proof support from those
148   tags. Appendix B records the role, supported scope, and appropriate evidence boundary for each
149   family.

150   3.2   Translation, solving, reconstruction, and reuse

151   Each backend request binds its family, premises, assumptions, target obligation, supported fragment,
152   and resource limits. Z3 and cvc5 supply SMT results, models, or cores; Vampire and E serve first-
153   order automated proving paths; interactive theorem-prover adapters support proof reconstruction and
154   kernel checking. Hammers select premises, translate to TPTP or SMT-LIB, run candidate-producing
155   portfolios, and reconstruct a native proof where supported. Solver success and kernel acceptance
156   remain different evidence kinds.
157   The verification API also exposes abstract interpretation, assume-guarantee discharge, incremental
158   SMT, differential checking, interpolation, and counterexample handling. These are selected methods,
159   not a checklist run on every invocation. For example, the inspected interpolation path is bounded to
160   QF_LIA with cvc5 production and independent checks of both interpolation conditions and shared
161   vocabulary. An unavailable interpolant can produce a separately labeled validated core; it cannot be
162   reported as a successful interpolant.
163   Proof-grounded IR learning uses source/IR, translation, reconstruction, proof, tactic, and coun-
164   terexample traces. Its contracts distinguish structural equality, logical equivalence, entailment,
165   equisatisfiability, and heuristic similarity. A learned encoder can nominate a representation or
166   premise set; the declared checker supplies acceptance evidence. Translation and reconstruction
167   receipts retain the weakest relevant boundary. A nearest-neighbor score, a successful paraphrase, or a
168   corpus-membership proof cannot upgrade a candidate to a theorem.


169   4     Joining intent, law, and security before execution

170   4.1   Concrete invocation and hard-constraint admission

171   An InvocationIntentEnvelope commits to source identity, tenant, actor, audience, delegation,
172   tool/version, arguments, proposed effects, environment, nonce, and relevant roots. Trusted adapters
173   bind the effect description to the actual operation. Otherwise an agent could authorize a harmless
174   description and execute a different effect.
175   Legal and security applicability are evaluated independently. Legal queries bind jurisdiction, subject,
176   authority, time, actor, resource, definitions, cross-references, exceptions, and source integrity. Security
177   queries bind principal, capability, trust zone, asset, channel, action, effect, failure/rollback, and
178   environment evidence. Hard filters precede ranking. Missing or contradictory required evidence is
179   retained as a blocking condition rather than removed to make a request pass.
180   The supervisor’s IRConstraintCompiler joins these results against the exact candidate action/effect
181   graph. It rejects undeclared effects, unmatched domain bindings, incomplete legal applicability,
182   applicable prohibitions, unresolved obligations, denied/unknown/conflicting security decisions, stale
183   roots, unsatisfied dependencies, missing proofs, and failed validation. Intent conformance and legal
184   permission constrain the candidate but do not grant security authorization. The output includes
185   rejection witnesses and the affected action set for local replanning rather than a scalar score that a
186   model can optimize around.

187   4.2   Authorization proof jobs and decision receipts

188   AuthorizationQueryComposer generates per-action jobs for applicability, positive grant, non-conflict,
189   security invariant, pre-obligation, coverage, and context binding. Coverage is relative to the declared
190   modeled scope, not proof that all relevant law has been collected. Additional jobs can address consis-
191   tency, translation, reconstruction, and obligations during or after execution. AuthorizationPortfolio
192   records attempts and selects compatible results deterministically; contradictory authoritative results
193   require review.
194   IntentAuthorizationService composes normalization, lowering, evidence selection, jobs, portfolio exe-
195   cution, decision, receipt, and optional internal capability derivation. It does not execute the requested
196   tool. Unknown, unsupported, contradictory, SAT-only, simulated, and merely policy/evidence-ready
197   outcomes cannot silently satisfy the proof-oriented allow policy. An AttestedProofVerifier checks
198   selected evidence against exact statements, assumptions, source/build/toolchain identities, obligations,


                                                          5
199   scope, and corpus/policy/revocation roots. Signed assertions and zero-knowledge artifacts retain the
200   statement and verifier they actually attest.

201   4.3   Pre-invocation enforcement

202   A receipt becomes operational only through a protected effect boundary. SupervisorPreInvocationEn-
203   forcement.authorize_and_delegate in enforce mode rechecks the live invocation, outcome, effects,
204   expiry, roots, and capability, then atomically consumes the permitted use before invoking the delegate.
205   Off, audit, and shadow modes do not provide the same prevention semantics. The supplied clock,
206   roots, receipt issuer, and consumption store must be trusted and appropriate to the deployment.
207   The separate short-lived ExecutionPermit path also binds the admitted graph, roots, mandatory
208   dependency closure, validation plan, caller, policy, lease, fence, expiry, and use bound. Plan admission
209   is not execution permission, and permission is not completion evidence. These interfaces must be
210   traced at each entry point: a lightweight Profile D dispatcher, a UCAN-gated MCP server, and a
211   full proof-oriented wrapper have related but nonidentical checks. Our security claim concerns a
212   configured, mediated path, not every historical compatibility route.

213   5     DuckDB and event-driven operational state

214   The supervisor’s mutable control state is DuckDB, specifically control.duckdb, behind the typed
215   Quack state-owner path. This is distinct from immutable IPFS artifacts and from read-only source-
216   corpus containers. DuckDB/Quack provides the database access substrate [5]; the implementation
217   adds ownership, command validation, generation/fence checks, and bounded retries.
218   QuackStateClient exposes closed, named parameter-bound commands rather than model-supplied
219   SQL. The inspected client supports an embedded exclusive-lock mode for hermetic/single-process
220   tooling and a Quack-mode typed Unix-socket owner gateway. It verifies store/server identity, schema
221   fingerprint, generation, and extension fingerprint. Generic client ATTACH and arbitrary SQL are not
222   the model-facing interface. The operational boundary is the project’s typed service, not unrestricted
223   access to every feature supported by the underlying Quack extension.
224   StateTransaction binds expected store generation, fence epoch, row revision, and idempotency
225   identity in short transactions. Same-row optimistic conflicts can receive bounded jittered retries;
226   stale generation, mismatched fence, and changed-payload idempotency conflicts are non-retryable
227   authority failures. A lost reply resolves against the committed command identity rather than causing
228   the LLM to repeat an effect. Database transitions are authoritative after commit. Event/outbox/cursor
229   integration and recovery must be qualified for the selected owner deployment; they are not replaced
230   by Markdown task-board edits.
231   Provider processes do not receive the owner’s token or an open SQL capability. The threat model
232   prohibits extension installation, file-reader escapes, cross-root operations, and Python UDF surfaces
233   through the typed client. Offline maintenance requires exclusive ownership. This is a single-owner
234   failure domain, not multi-primary consensus. DuckLake projections or exported reports can support
235   analysis but do not acquire task-mutation authority.
236   Source, proof, policy, capability, and completion events trigger rescanning and targeted invalidation.
237   Relevant changes reopen affected obligations; unchanged semantic dependencies remain candidates
238   for reuse. The supervisor’s generation and fencing mechanisms prevent an old worker from publishing
239   its result over newer state. Durable capability consumption and uncertain remote-effect recovery
240   must be tested in the actual executor: a DuckDB commit alone does not make an arbitrary remote
241   API exactly once.

242   6     IPFS, libp2p, UCAN, and MCP++ in the execution path

243   6.1   IPFS: identity and distribution of evidence

244   The InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) uses content addressing to name bytes by content rather than
245   by a mutable location [6]. Kit stores source bodies, IR declarations, graphs, ContextPacks, proofs,
246   source/alias maps, decisions, and receipts as immutable artifacts. A recipient rehashes the retrieved


                                                         6
247   bytes under the declared profile. Canonical declarations bind domain and schema so equal-looking
248   payloads from different families cannot substitute for one another.
249   The IR kernel uses a declared canonical-JSON identity envelope and CIDv1/raw/SHA-256 profile;
250   other contracts, including Profile D artifacts, use DAG-JSON. The codec and preimage are part
251   of the contract. A plain SHA-256 digest is not relabeled a CID. A graph root commits to its
252   referenced closure, while mutable current roots advance through a controlled publication path. This
253   enables reproducible evidence reuse across machines. Availability still requires retention/pinning and
254   functioning peers; local CID computation proves neither publication nor public availability. Private
255   evidence needs explicit access and confidentiality controls.


256   6.2   libp2p: peer communication, not permission

257   libp2p supplies peer identities, transport selection, secure channels, protocol negotiation, and multi-
258   plexed streams [7]. A Peer ID identifies a key-bound peer; it is not an application user or a tool grant.
259   The MCP++ P2P carriage profile uses /mcp+p2p/1.0.0 with framed MCP messages. Stdio, HTTP,
260   and P2P adapters should converge on the same validated dispatch semantics rather than implement
261   different authorization policies.
262   The relevant architectural benefit is that another agent can exchange an objective, request an IR
263   artifact by CID, obtain proof evidence, or call a permitted tool without sharing the supervisor database.
264   Discovery or a successfully authenticated peer connection does not authorize those operations. Tests
265   of a local stream framing path also must not be reported as a wide-area network or adversarial
266   transport qualification.


267   6.3   UCAN: attenuated delegated authority

268   The User Controlled Authorization Network (UCAN) represents signed delegated capabilities [8]. In
269   the inspected strict verifier, Ed25519 signatures, issuer/audience continuity, parent proofs, explicit
270   resource/ability fields, validity windows, and supported request bounds are checked. A child capability
271   must remain within the parent’s authority. A complete path segment matters: tenant-a/* must not
272   accidentally include tenant-ab. The durable revocation/replay ledger supplies state that a signature
273   alone cannot provide.
274   This is separate from a formal proof. UCAN answers whether an authenticated principal may exercise
275   a delegated capability. Legal and security reasoning answer whether this particular operation satisfies
276   the applicable modeled constraints. Both must be satisfied where required. A parsed unsigned
277   delegation stub, an agent-authored permission sentence, or a valid signature on irrelevant bytes cannot
278   replace the strict verifier. The proof-derived AuthorizationCapability and a signed UCAN token are
279   also distinct formats; deployments must connect them through an explicit trusted binding rather than
280   equating their names.


281   6.4   MCP++: where these mechanisms meet

282   MCP++ adds optional profiles around MCP rather than replacing JSON-RPC. Profile A identifies the
283   interface; B binds invocation and result artifacts; C specifies delegation; D carries temporal-deontic
284   policy; E carries P2P messages; F records causal provenance; and G describes tasks, claims, leases,
285   and coordination. Their portable schemas support heterogeneous agents, while the implementations
286   perform the checks.
287   Kit’s protected tools/call route invokes AuthorizationGate before dispatch. The gate checks enve-
288   lope/tool/resource/ability bindings, a concrete UCAN verifier and ledger, and the Datasets-backed
289   Profile D provider, then records the decision. Missing validators or required authorities deny. Profile
290   D matches actor/action/resource/time, applies prohibition precedence, and requires a matching permis-
291   sion. A returned obligation needs a handling policy; the inspected strict Kit gate accepts plain allow,
292   not unresolved allow_with_obligations. This is a concrete policy gate. Stronger theorem-required
293   actions additionally need the proof-oriented enforcement path; carrying a proof_cid in an output
294   envelope does not establish that it was checked before execution.

295   7   Worked source-to-effect trace and evaluation
296   Consider an agent that retrieves a Markdown procedure for exporting records. The following is a
297   composition walkthrough, not a newly executed integration result. The source record contributes
298   a procedure and source spans, not permission. Intent IR describes the selected records, recipi-
299   ent/channel, expected effects, prerequisites, and verification. The legal slice supplies applicable
300   reviewed disclosure/retention constraints. A retrieved vulnerability/fix example contributes a scoped
301   candidate constraint for an unsafe data-flow or missing validation; applicability and review determine
302   whether it participates. Generated code is independently analyzed so that its effects can be correlated
303   with the stated intent.
304   The supervisor binds this candidate to current Legal/Security/Intent roots and composes the required
305   proof jobs. An unapproved destination, an unresolved exception, or an undeclared code effect blocks
306   admission. A permissible candidate still requires a current grant for the exact actor, tool, resource,
307   and bounds. The protected runtime checks the receipt and capability immediately before delegation;
308   a recipient change invalidates the argument commitment, while revocation or consumed-use state
309   rejects replay. DuckDB records operational progression, and immutable evidence records explain
310   which sources, rules, proofs, and grants led to the result. The model can propose a revised plan, but it
311   cannot remove a failed obligation by rewriting its explanation.
312   The evaluation should exercise these existing paths. Source tests cover bounded SkillCenter reading,
313   deterministic identities, CVE scope and authority rejection, typed compilation, proof-result authority,
314   enforce-mode non-delegation, replay/concurrent consumption, Quack command/fence checks, and
315   MCP transport parity. Inspected test assertions are not executed results. The next frozen experiment
316   must record actual results separately for fixture, local solver, cryptographic, storage, and end-to-end
317   agent paths.
318   Compare unguarded sandbox execution, retrieval plus prompt instructions, lightweight policy/UCAN
319   gating, and full proof-oriented enforcement on the same handlers. Cross-source cases should
320   include malicious skill text, a wrong-jurisdiction or stale legal version, a misleading CVE match,
321   vulnerable/fixed controls, omitted code effects, a widened capability, changed arguments, and restarted
322   state. Measure actual forbidden delegate effects together with allowed-task success, false denial,
323   abstention, source/formalization fidelity, route coverage, tokens, proof cost, and latency. No numerical
324   safety or cost advantage is asserted before these runs. Appendices provide the lineage and experiment
325   fields.

326   8   Scope and conclusion
327   The implemented contribution is a source-grounded constraint pipeline and a controllable action
328   boundary. It does not require a complete global corpus to enforce a reviewed bounded policy, and a
329   large corpus does not prove completeness. Formalization may be partial; model proposals, signatures,
330   content identities, monitor observations, solver results, and kernel proofs retain distinct meanings.
331   Complete mediation, trusted current roots, authenticated issuers, and appropriate persistence remain
332   deployment requirements.
333   The system is best understood as a compiler-and-runtime stack: Legal IR supplies normative con-
334   straints, Security IR supplies behavior and threat constraints, and Intent IR supplies proposed
335   procedures. Native logics and proof tools determine what evidence is needed; DuckDB maintains
336   operational truth; IPFS preserves exact artifacts; libp2p carries peer interactions; and UCAN limits
337   delegated authority. The safety-relevant result is not a more confident explanation. It is whether a
338   non-admitted action is prevented from reaching a protected handler.

456   B.2   Proof workflow and result classes

457   The common proof workflow is: choose a supported view; create a typed obligation and premises;
458   select a backend/hammer plan; execute within a bound; preserve translations, models, cores, and
459   proof candidates; reconstruct where required; run the declared checker; then emit a result and receipt.
460   Z3/cvc5, Vampire/E, and Lean/Coq/Isabelle belong to different stages. A timeout or unavailable
461   backend is not a refutation, and satisfiability of a favorable instance does not prove universal safety.
462   Datasets distinguishes authority kinds such as theorem proof, satisfiability, runtime monitor, evidence
463   readiness, and policy approval. These are non-interchangeable. The supervisor also uses an assurance
464   classification for whether the evidence meets a requested threshold. Neither mechanism permits a
465   provider to upgrade its own output by naming it “verified.” A cryptographic attestation must bind the
466   actual checked claim, verifier and public inputs; proving corpus membership alone cannot prove the
467   member’s theorem.
468   Counterexample-guided refinement identifies missing facts or assumptions in the encoded problem
469   and requests specific additional context. Interpolation and unsat cores can narrow that request; they
470   do not discover an omitted statute automatically. Abstract interpretation overapproximates a qualified
471   program fragment, while assume-guarantee discharge checks that a producer’s established guarantee
472   entails a consumer’s assumption. Learned premise/tactic selection and proof-grounded encoders
473   improve search proposals; independent checking remains the acceptance operation.

476   C.2   A state transition through the typed owner

477   A caller supplies a named command and typed parameters, not a SQL string. The owner validates
478   store identity, schema/generation, task revision, fence, and idempotency key. A successful transaction
479   commits the permitted state change and its recorded command result. The identical command can
480   retrieve that result after a lost reply; a changed payload with the same identity is a conflict. Stale
481   generations and fences require new admission rather than blind retry. Event delivery can wake
482   dependent work, but does not substitute for the committed state.
483   The inspected QuackStateClient describes both an embedded mode and a typed owner-gateway mode.
484   Documentation that discusses generic Quack/ATTACH capability must not override the current
485   client’s deliberately narrower interface. Likewise, the architecture should not promise that a reference
486   in-memory capability consumption store is already backed by DuckDB. A deployment must supply
487   and test the actual durable adapter used for receipt/capability consumption.

488   C.3   IPFS and libp2p are complementary

489   IPFS answers which artifact bytes are requested and enables verifiable retrieval of that content.
490   Canonical IR, a source document, a decision receipt, and a graph node can each have independent
491   identities. Pin/retention and transport choices determine availability. Public availability is not the
492   default requirement for private legal or security material.
493   libp2p answers how peers connect, authenticate a peer key, negotiate an application protocol, and
494   exchange framed streams. A protocol ID selects the decoder; it does not select a legal jurisdiction
495   or an authorization profile by itself. TLS/Noise or native transport security protects a channel when
496   configured; it does not turn a peer’s proof claim into a checked proof. Deployments must record
497   actual transports and security settings. Do not count an in-process call to a P2P handler as a full
498   libp2p network experiment.

499   C.4   UCAN and proof-oriented capabilities are distinct

500   Strict UCAN checks cryptographic issuer identity, delegation continuity, explicit resource/ability
501   bounds, temporal validity, permitted attenuation, revocation, and replay policy. It does not infer
502   delegation from natural-language text. A principal may possess a valid capability yet fail a legal or
503   security obligation. Conversely, a mathematically valid proof of a property does not authorize the
504   principal to invoke an administrative tool.
505   The proof-oriented AuthorizationCapability is an additional exact-context contract. Its relationship
506   to a UCAN envelope must bind the same actor/audience, tool/version, arguments, effects, policy
507   roots, and allowed use. Hash equality checks identity; authentication must come from the trusted
508   issuer/transport mechanism. A signed token, capability use record, and proof receipt are related
509   evidence objects, not aliases for one another.
